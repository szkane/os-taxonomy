🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🌍Where water is found on Earth — Identify where water is found on Earth and understand that water can exist as solid (ice) or liquid, recognising water in oceans, rivers, glaciers, and underground
  • 🌍Properties of materials — Compare and group different kinds of rocks based on their appearance and simple physical properties such as hardness, texture, and colour
  • 🌍How fossils form — Describe in simple terms how fossils are formed when things that have lived are trapped within rock over millions of years
  • 🌍Preventing Erosion — Compare multiple solutions designed to slow or prevent wind or water from changing the shape of the land
  • 🌍Evaporation and condensation — Name and use vocabulary for the water cycle — evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection, transpiration, water vapour, runoff, groundwater — and describe each …
  • 🌍Rocks and soil — Recognise that soils are made from rocks and organic matter, and that different soils have different properties
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning about Earth's structure — exploring different types of rocks and soil, understanding where water is found on our planet, and discovering how land shapes change over time.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Where water is found on Earth'Most of Earth's water is fresh / in rivers and lakes'Next step: Compare the volumes — oceans dwarf rivers and lakes; most of Earth's water is salty.
Properties of materials'All rocks are basically the same (hard and grey)'Next step: Handle a varied set — feel hardness, texture and weight, see the range of colours.
How fossils form'A fossil is just the actual original bone or shell, unchanged'Next step: The shape is preserved but the material is often replaced and turned to rock over time.
Preventing ErosionUnfair test — pours different amounts or from different heights, then compares the traysNext step: Model controlling variables — keep everything the same except the solution being tested.
Evaporation and condensationSwaps 'evaporation' and 'condensation' ('the cloud evaporates into rain')Next step: Anchor it: evaporation = liquid → vapour (going up, drying); condensation = vapour → liquid (cloud/droplets forming).
Rocks and soil'Soil has always just been there and isn't made of anything'Next step: Soil forms as rock weathers and organisms decompose, over a very long time.
