🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍕Equivalent fractions (age 9+) — Explain why a fraction a/b is equivalent to (n×a)/(n×b) using visual models; use this principle to recognise and generate equivalent fractions, including tenths and …
  • 🍕Decimals for Tenths & Hundredths — Use decimal notation for fractions with denominators 10 or 100; read and write decimal numbers as fractions (e.g. 0.62 = 62/100, 0.71 = 71/100)
  • 🍕Converting tenths to hundredths — Express a fraction with denominator 10 as an equivalent fraction with denominator 100 and use this to add fractions with denominators 10 and 100 (e.g. …
  • 🍕Tenths (age 9+) — Recognise and use thousandths; relate them to tenths, hundredths, and their decimal equivalents (e.g. 1/1000 = 0.001, 35/1000 = 0.035)
  • 🍕Understanding fractions (age 9+) — Understand a fraction a/b with a > 1 as a sum of fractions 1/b (e.g. 3/5 = 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5)
  • 🍕Comparing Decimals — Compare two decimals to hundredths (or up to three decimal places) by reasoning about size using place-value understanding; record with >, =, <
  • 🍕Multiplying fractions — Understand a/b as a multiple of 1/b; multiply proper fractions and mixed numbers by whole numbers, supported by visual models (e.g. 3 × 2/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5)
  • 🍕Percentage and decimal equivalents — Solve problems requiring knowledge of percentage and decimal equivalents of 1/2, 1/4, 1/5, 2/5, 4/5 and fractions with denominators that are multiples of 10 or 25
  • 🍕Understanding Percentages — Understand the per cent symbol (%); know that per cent means ‘number of parts per hundred’; write percentages as a fraction with denominator 100 and as a decimal
  • 🍕Adding fractions (different denominators) — Add and subtract fractions with denominators that are multiples of the same number by finding a common denominator
  • 🍕Fraction Addition Concepts — Understand addition and subtraction of fractions as joining and separating parts; decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator in more than one …
  • 🍕Mixed numbers and improper fractions — Recognise mixed numbers and improper fractions; convert from one form to the other (e.g. 2/5 + 4/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5)
  • 🍕Comparing fractions (age 9+) — Compare and order fractions with different numerators and denominators by creating common denominators/numerators or comparing to a benchmark such as 1/2; justify …
  • 🍕Decimals to three places — Solve problems involving numbers with up to three decimal places
  • 🍕Adding and subtracting mixed numbers — Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, including by converting to improper fractions or using properties of operations
  • 🍕Addition and subtraction word problems — Solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions with like denominators, using visual models and equations
  • 🍕Fractions of a whole (age 9+) — Solve word problems involving multiplication of a fraction by a whole number using visual models and equations
  • 🍕Decimal place value (age 9+) — Round decimals with two decimal places to the nearest whole number and to one decimal place
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring fractions, decimals, and percentages — understanding how they relate to each other, adding and subtracting fractions, and solving real-world problems involving these concepts.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Equivalent fractions (age 9+)Additive equivalence — 'you can add the same number to top and bottom and it stays equal, so 2/3 = 3/4'Next step: Place 2/3 and 3/4 on one number line to show different points; contrast splitting each part (×) with shifting the amount (+)
Decimals for Tenths & HundredthsDrops the placeholder zero — reads 0.07 as 7/10, or writes 9/100 as 0.9 — mapping the digit straight after the point.Next step: Use the place-value chart / grid: the 9 belongs in the hundredths (second) place, so a zero holds the tenths place; contrast 9/10 = 0.9 with 9/100 = 0.09. Reteach that place fixes the denominator.
Converting tenths to hundredthsAdds across both denominators: 'three tenths plus four hundredths is seven over a hundred-and-ten' — combines numerators and denominators as if they were whole numbersNext step: Return to the grid and count: one column is ten squares, so 3/10 covers thirty squares. You can only add once the pieces are the same size. Rebuild the equivalence 3/10 = 30/100 before re-adding.
Tenths (age 9+)Whole-number bias: 'a thousandth is bigger than a hundredth because 1000 is bigger than 100'.Next step: Lay one thousandth against one hundredth so the thousandth is visibly tiny; re-establish 'more, smaller parts means each is smaller'. Reteach the tenfold place structure from tenths/hundredths.
Understanding fractions (age 9+)Believes a fraction must be less than 1, so 7/4 'isn't a proper number' or must be a mistake — stops the jumps at 1.Next step: Iterate 1/4 aloud past 4/4 on the line: 5/4, 6/4, 7/4 — each is just one more quarter and lands on a definite point. Reteach that a/b counts copies of 1/b, so the count can exceed the denominator.
Comparing DecimalsLonger-is-larger: 'more digits means bigger — 0.38 is more than 0.4 because 38 is bigger than 4', treating the digits after the point as a whole number.Next step: Show the length model — 0.38 is shorter than 0.4 — and reason in place value: '0.38 is 3 tenths 8 hundredths, less than 4 tenths'. Use ragged decimals; name and inhibit the whole-number rule. Reteach comparison of same-length decimals first.
