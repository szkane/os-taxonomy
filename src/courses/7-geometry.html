🎯 What you’ll learn

  • ⬡Right Angles & Turns — Identify right angles; recognise that two right angles make a half-turn, three make three-quarters, and four make a complete turn
  • ⬡Parallel and perpendicular lines — Identify horizontal and vertical lines and pairs of perpendicular and parallel lines
  • ⬡Recognising and drawing shapes by attributes — Recognise and draw shapes having specified attributes (e.g. a given number of angles or equal faces); identify triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, and cubes
  • ⬡Congruent shapes by matching — Identify congruent side lengths and congruent angles in two-dimensional shapes by matching them physically (tracing, overlaying, folding, comparing with a strip) or …
  • ⬡2-D shapes (age 7+) — Draw 2-D shapes and make 3-D shapes using modelling materials; recognise 3-D shapes in different orientations and describe them
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning about shapes, angles, and lines — identifying different types of angles and lines, drawing 2D shapes and building 3D models, and understanding how angles work as turns and properties of shapes.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Right Angles & TurnsA right angle must point up like an L; a tilted or mirrored one is not a right angle ('that one's on its side, so it's different')Next step: Rotate the checker itself onto the tilted corner so it still fits exactly — show the right angle is preserved under rotation; have the child re-test each corner with the checker turned to match.
Parallel and perpendicular linesThinks parallel lines must be the SAME LENGTH — says the tilted unequal pair is not parallel because the lines differ in lengthNext step: Show parallel pairs of very different lengths; define parallel by constant distance / never meeting, tested by extending — length is irrelevant (NCETM).
Recognising and drawing shapes by attributesDraws or accepts only the prototype — a regular, symmetrical, horizontal-base example — and misses irregular or rotated members of the class.Next step: Define the class by its attribute (a quadrilateral is ANY closed shape with four straight sides), then present many varied and irregular examples with non-examples to sort, and ask for a 'different one that doesn't look like that'.
2-D shapes (age 7+)Fails to recognise a solid (or 2-D shape) in an unfamiliar orientation — says a rotated or point-down pyramid is 'a different shape' or 'not a pyramid'.Next step: Let them handle and physically rotate the solid so they see it is the same object throughout; name it by properties (triangular faces meeting at an apex) that do not change with orientation.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Ask the child to PREDICT how many right-angle (quarter) turns it takes to end up facing exactly the way they started, and to say WHY, before doing it. Then have them make the turns one quarter at a time, counting a right angle each time and noticing where they face after two. …

Activity ideas

  • Make and use a right-angle checker
  • Build turns from right angles
  • Sort and classify mixed angles
  • Extend and rotate: what parallel really means
