🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🥚Times tables (age 7+) — Recall and use multiplication and division facts for the 3, 4, and 8 multiplication tables
  • 🥚Arrays for multiplication (age 7+) — Use addition to find the total number of objects arranged in rectangular arrays with up to 5 rows and 5 columns; write an equation to express the total as a sum of equal …
  • 🥚Written Multiplication & Division — Write and calculate mathematical statements for multiplication and division using known tables, including two-digit × one-digit, using mental and progressing to formal …
  • 🥚Multi-Step Multiply & Divide — Solve problems involving multiplication and division, including scaling problems and correspondence problems where n objects are connected to m objects
  • 🥚Rows & Columns in Rectangles — Partition a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares and count to find the total number of them
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering multiplication and division — learning their times tables (especially 3, 4, and 8), understanding how multiplication connects to arrays and repeated addition, and solving word problems involving these operations.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Times tables (age 7+)isolated facts, no derivation — knows some 4s but treats the 8s as a separate list, unable to use 4 × 6 to reach 8 × 6Next step: teach doubling (8 = double 4) and ring every other multiple of 4 to reveal the 8s; make the relationship explicit
Arrays for multiplication (age 7+)adds the dimensions, not the equal rows — writes something like 3 + 4 (row-count plus row-size) instead of repeated equal addendsNext step: shade one row (4), then show it repeats 3 times → 4 + 4 + 4; distinguish the number of rows from the amount in each row
Written Multiplication & Divisionmultiplies only one part — does 20 × 4 = 80 and forgets 3 × 4, giving 80; or multiplies the digits as if 2 and 3Next step: use an area model / grid: a 23-by-4 rectangle split into a 20-by-4 and a 3-by-4 region; both regions must be counted and added.
Multi-Step Multiply & Divideadds on the correspondence problem (4 + 3 = 7 outfits) instead of pairing every shirt with every trouserNext step: lay out the pairings systematically (each shirt with each of 3 trousers) so 4 groups of 3 = 12 becomes visible.

Activity ideas

  • The doubling chain: 2s → 4s → 8s
  • One fact gives four: multiplication and division fact families
  • Out-of-order recall and targeting the sticky facts
  • Sum the rows: build the equal-addend equation from an array
