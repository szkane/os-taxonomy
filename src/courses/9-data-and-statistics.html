🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 📊Reading tables — Complete, read, and interpret information in tables, including timetables
  • 📊Statistical Analysis Vocabulary — Read, write, and use the vocabulary of statistical analysis — mean, median, mode, range, frequency, data, sample, average, chart, table, graph, pie chart, scatter graph, …
  • 📊Line graph problems — Solve comparison, sum, and difference problems using information presented in a line graph
  • 📊Comparing displays and distributions — Analyse how effectively different displays or visualisations show and compare data distributions, and describe the shape of a distribution and the variation in the data
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning to work with data — reading timetables and graphs, and solving problems by interpreting the information presented in charts and line graphs.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Reading tablesTime-as-decimal: computes a duration by subtracting or reading clock times as base-10 numbers — giving 0.70 for 10:50 to 11:20, or calling a journey '2.23 hours' — because minutes are treated as tenths rather than sixtiethsNext step: Reteach duration on the time-conversion prerequisite: use an analogue clock and empty number line, establish 60 minutes in an hour, and count on 10:50→11:00→11:20 so the child sees the answer as minutes, not tenths
Statistical Analysis Vocabulary'Average' as bare procedure: uses 'average'/'mean' only for the add-and-divide calculation and cannot say what the resulting number represents about the dataNext step: Confront by pressing for meaning: 'if the team scored this average every match, how many is that?' — tie the mean to a fair-share / typical value on the actual data, so the word carries a meaning, not just a rule
Line graph problemsGraph-as-picture: reads the line's shape as a literal picture of the situation rather than a quantity over time ('it goes up like a hill then down the other side', or describes it as a route/mountain) instead of talking about temperature values on daysNext step: Confront directly: cover the whole line and expose one point at a time, asking 'what does THIS point tell us?' and tracing it to both axes, until the child re-reads the line as a set of day/temperature pairs rather than a picture

Activity ideas

  • Live timetable with intersection highlighting
  • Confront time-as-decimal with an analogue clock
  • Complete and interpret a two-way table
  • Scorable checks on lookups, durations and cell completion
