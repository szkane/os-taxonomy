Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Where Are the Poles?'The North Pole is cold because the top of the Earth is closest to the Sun / space' — muddles 'top' of the globe with nearness to the Sun.Next step: Show that the whole Earth is roughly the same distance from the Sun; the poles are cold because they are farthest from the warm middle (Equator) where the Sun's warmth is strongest.
Arctic vs Antarctic'Polar bears and penguins live together / could meet' — the storybook-and-advert image of bears with penguins.Next step: Put bears at the top of the globe and penguins at the bottom; they are worlds apart and never share a home. Show it on a map.
Polar Animals'Animals just endure the cold / shiver through it' — no notion of adaptation.Next step: Introduce features: fat, thick fur/feathers, colour change. Match each animal to how its body helps it, like a built-in coat.
Penguins'Penguins live at the North Pole / with polar bears' — opposite-poles confusion.Next step: Re-anchor: penguins = far South (Antarctic). Point to the bottom of the globe; bears are at the top.
Polar Bears'Polar bears live at the South Pole too / near penguins' — opposite-poles confusion.Next step: Re-anchor: polar bears = Arctic (North) only. Point to the top of the globe.
Ice & Snow'Heavy/big things always sink' — judges floating by size or weight, not by comparison to water.Next step: Show a big ice cube floating in a bowl; floating depends on being lighter than the SAME amount of water, not on how big it is.
