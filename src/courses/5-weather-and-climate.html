📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is discovering how weather works — learning about different types of weather, how to stay safe during storms, and understanding how weather patterns change with the seasons.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Types of WeatherWeather = only rain or sun — 'if nothing is falling there's no weather'Next step: Widen the category: cloudy, windy, foggy are all weather too; ask what the sky and air are doing, not just what's falling.
Seasons & Weather PatternsSeason = today's weather — 'it's cold, so it must be winter'Next step: Stress duration and pattern: a season lasts months and has TYPICAL weather; one odd day doesn't move the calendar. Track a week to show the season holds.
Rain & PuddlesEvaporation as disappearance — 'it dried up and is just gone'Next step: Establish conservation: the water changed into invisible vapour and is still there; capture it under a lid to show it comes back as droplets.
Temperature & ThermometersTouch is the true temperature — 'the metal is colder because it feels colder'Next step: Contrast feel with measurement: leave both in the same room, measure, find them equal; the metal just draws heat from your hand faster so it feels colder.
What Is Wind?Reversed causality — 'trees (or waves, or leaves) make the wind by moving'Next step: Flip the cause: show a windless day where nothing waves, then a fan (moving air) making a still branch wave — the air moves the object.
Weather Forecasting & SafetyForecaster causes the weather — 'saying it will rain makes it rain'Next step: Separate telling from causing: the forecaster reads clues to guess what's coming, like guessing the score before a game — saying it doesn't make it happen.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Put a shallow puddle of water in a saucer, cover it with cling film, and leave it in a sunny spot. Ask children to predict what will happen to the water and WHY — many predict it will simply dry up and be gone. …

Activity ideas

  • Sensory pairing: weather you feel as well as see
  • Build today's weather report and tally the week
  • Match and describe: check the vocabulary
