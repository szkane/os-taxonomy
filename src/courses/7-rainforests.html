Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Rainforest Water Cycleall rain blows in from the sea/sky — the forest is a passive place that just gets rained onNext step: introduce transpiration; the forest releases moisture that becomes its own clouds and rain
Classifying Rainforest Organismssorts by what an animal does or where it lives, not its features — 'it flies so it's a bird'Next step: switch the sorting rule to observable body features; fur+milk means mammal regardless of flying
Rainforest Food Websthe arrow points to the food/prey — 'it points at what gets eaten'Next step: the arrow means 'energy flows to'; it points to the EATER, from food to feeder
Tropical Rainforest Climatehot is what matters; a hot place is a hot place — doesn't see rainfall as the key differenceNext step: contrast rainfall totals; heat + heavy rain = rainforest, heat + little rain = desert
The Amazon Rainforestthe Amazon is a country / only in Brazil — confuses the forest with a nationNext step: it's a rainforest spanning nine South American countries; locate it on a map
Indigenous Ecological Knowledgeall forest clearing is the same destruction — 'they cut trees, so they're destroying it too'Next step: contrast scale and permanence; a small plot left to regrow is a sustainable cycle

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Seal a clear plastic bag over a leafy branch of a living plant (leave it in the light) and ask the child to predict, WITH A REASON, whether any water will appear inside. The naive prediction ('no — where would water come from, it hasn't rained?') is the diagnostic part. …

Activity ideas

  • Run and perturb the forest's rain loop
  • Label the loop and name each stage
  • Predict, test and explain: the bag-on-a-branch investigation
  • Order the stages and use the key word
