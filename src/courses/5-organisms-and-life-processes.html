🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🦋Naming Common Animals — Identify and name common animals from major groups: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals
  • 🦋Common Plants & Trees — Identify and name common wild and garden plants, including deciduous and evergreen trees
  • 🦋Living Things Vocabulary — Name and use vocabulary for what makes something living — alive, dead, never been alive, movement, nutrition, growth, reproduction, sensitivity, excretion — and apply …
  • 🦋What Living Things Need — Understand what plants and animals (including humans) need to survive: water, food, air, and suitable conditions
  • 🦋Herbivores, Carnivores & Omnivores — Classify common animals as carnivores (eat meat), herbivores (eat plants), or omnivores (eat both)
  • 🦋Animal Body Groups — Describe and compare the external body structure of common animals across groups (fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals)
  • 🦋Parts of a Plant — Identify and describe the basic structure of common flowering plants (roots, stem, leaves, flowers) and trees (roots, trunk, branches, leaves)
  • 🦋Body Parts & Senses — Identify, name, and locate basic parts of the human body and associate each body part with its sense
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning about the amazing diversity of life — identifying different animals and plants, understanding how body parts help animals survive, and discovering what all living things need to stay alive.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Naming Common AnimalsIf it lives in water it's a fish ('the dolphin swims in the sea, so it's a fish')Next step: Focus on features: it surfaces to breathe air and feeds its babies milk. Group by body characteristics, not where the animal lives.
Common Plants & TreesA bare tree in winter is dead ('the oak's got no leaves, it must have died')Next step: Distinguish dormant from dead: scratch the twig to show green underneath, point to buds already waiting; it will leaf again in spring. Confront directly with a spring photo of the same tree.
Living Things Vocabularythings that move are alive and things that don't are not ('the car is alive because it moves; the plant isn't because it doesn't')Next step: shift the test from movement to the full set of life processes; a car moves but doesn't grow, feed or reproduce, while a plant does
What Living Things Needplants only really need water (light and air not essential)Next step: list all a plant's needs — water, light, air, suitable temperature — and show light is essential using a dark-grown seedling
Herbivores, Carnivores & Omnivorescarnivores are the big, fierce, scary animals and herbivores the small, gentle ones (sorts by appearance, not diet)Next step: give counterexamples — the elephant and cow are huge herbivores — and sort strictly by what each animal eats
Animal Body GroupsIf it flies it's a bird ('the bat has wings so it must be a bird')Next step: Regroup by body covering: the bat has fur and feeds its babies milk, so it's a mammal. Compare feathers vs fur side by side.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

In late winter, cut a few bare twigs from a deciduous tree (e.g. horse chestnut with sticky buds) and stand them in water on the windowsill. Ask each child to predict what the 'dead-looking stick' will do over two weeks and WHY. …
