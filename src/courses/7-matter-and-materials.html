🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧊Heating & Cooling Changes — Observe and describe that some materials change state when heated or cooled, and measure the temperature at which changes occur in degrees Celsius
  • 🧊Drawing Particle Diagrams — Draw and interpret particle diagrams — dot representations showing the arrangement, spacing, and movement of particles in solids (close, regular, vibrating in place), …
  • 🧊Testing Materials for Uses — Give reasons, based on evidence from comparative and fair tests, for the particular uses of everyday materials including metals, wood, and plastic
  • 🧊Changes & Separation Vocabulary — Use process vocabulary for changes of state and material separation — dissolve, solution, soluble, insoluble, evaporate, condense, melt, freeze, filter, sieve, mixture, …
  • 🧊Classifying Materials — Plan and conduct an investigation to classify different kinds of materials by their observable properties
  • 🧊Taking Apart & Rebuilding — Observe that an object made of a small set of pieces can be disassembled and made into a new object, understanding that the pieces still exist
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is investigating how materials behave — classifying substances by their properties, understanding why different materials are used for specific purposes, and exploring how heating and cooling can change materials.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Heating & Cooling ChangesMelting = dissolving — 'the ice dissolved into water'; the two processes are treated as the same.Next step: Contrast: melting needs only heat and involves one substance changing state; dissolving needs a SECOND substance (a liquid the solid spreads into) and no heating is required.
Drawing Particle Diagramsdraws the particles themselves changing size — bigger for gas, smaller for solid ('they spread out AND get bigger')Next step: Same object, same particles — only the SPACING and arrangement change. Have them redraw keeping every dot identical in size across all three states.
Testing Materials for UsesConclusion asserted from prior belief, not evidence — decides 'plastic wins' before testing and ignores a surprising result.Next step: Hide the material labels or engineer a surprising outcome, and insist the claim quote the measured result ('the tray under the paper had the most water').
Changes & Separation Vocabularyuses 'melt' for dissolving too ('the sugar melted in the water')Next step: Contrast the two events side by side: the lolly melts with NO water needed (just warmth); the sugar needs the water and doesn't need heat. Name each as it happens.
Classifying MaterialsClassifies from assumption, not results — 'all metals are magnetic', so groups metals together without genuinely testing, or overrides a 'no' from the aluminium.Next step: Have them slowly bring the magnet to the aluminium foil and copper coin and watch nothing happen; the recorded 'no' is the fact — not all metals are magnetic.
Taking Apart & RebuildingNon-conservation on rearrangement — thinks bricks are gained or lost when the object changes, or that a taller/bigger build 'has more'.Next step: Re-separate and re-count together; if a build looks bigger, spread the other into a line so 'looks more' is uncoupled from 'is more'.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Put a thermometer into a beaker of crushed melting ice and warm it gently, reading the temperature aloud. Ask the child to PREDICT what the thermometer does WHILE the ice is still melting, and WHY, before you start. Most predict it climbs steadily. …

Activity ideas

  • Heat-and-cool bench: watch the change track the temperature
  • Real thermometer in melting ice: predict the sticking point
  • Melting is not dissolving — and freezing is heat leaving
  • Every material has its own change temperatures
