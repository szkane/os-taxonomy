🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🥚Times tables — Recall and use multiplication and division facts for the 2, 5, and 10 multiplication tables
  • 🥚Commutative Multiplication — Understand and apply the commutative property of multiplication and recognise that division is not commutative
  • 🥚Reading ×, ÷, and = Symbols — Read, write, and interpret the symbols ×, ÷, and = in multiplication and division number sentences
  • 🥚Multiplication as repeated addition (age 6+) — Solve problems involving multiplication and division using arrays, repeated addition, mental methods, and known facts
  • 🥚Odd and even numbers — Recognise odd and even numbers
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is building the foundations of multiplication and division — learning their 2, 5, and 10 times tables, understanding what these operations mean, and solving problems using arrays and repeated addition.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Times tablesdivision facts lag multiplication — fluent on 5 × 3 but stalls on 20 ÷ 5; the ÷ fact isn't linked to the same fact familyNext step: teach fact families on one array/triangle — 5 × 4 = 20, so 20 ÷ 5 = 4 and 20 ÷ 4 = 5
Commutative Multiplicationover-generalises commutativity to division — thinks 12 ÷ 3 = 3 ÷ 12 because 'order doesn't matter in maths'Next step: act out 12 ÷ 3 (4 each) versus 3 ÷ 12 (sharing 3 among 12) with counters so the difference is felt; contrast with the rotate-the-array move for ×
Reading ×, ÷, and = Symbolsswaps × and ÷ — reads or writes one where the other is meant; the two symbols aren't distinguishedNext step: anchor each symbol to its action — × builds equal groups into a total, ÷ breaks the whole up — pairing symbol with picture repeatedly
Multiplication as repeated addition (age 6+)operation confusion — for '5 bags of 2' writes 5 + 2 = 7, combining the numbers rather than making equal groupsNext step: act out the groups with objects, count the total, and connect it to 2+2+2+2+2 and 5×2 so the groups-of structure is felt
Odd and even numbersdecides odd/even by counting-to-say only — knows the last-digit rule but cannot connect it to pairing; thinks 'even' just means 'a number you say when counting by twos'Next step: do NOT accept the digit rule as proof — have the child pair the counters physically and name 'even = everyone has a partner, odd = one left alone'; connect it back to why the digits work only after the pairing meaning is secure.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Set out 3 groups of 5 counters. Before building the comparison, ask the child to PREDICT: 'If I make 5 groups of 3 instead, will the total be more, fewer, or the same — and why?' They commit to a reason, then build 5 groups of 3 and count. …

Activity ideas

  • Shade the table: derive the 5s from the 10s
  • Four facts from one array: multiplication and its inverse
  • Mixed quick-recall, out of order, with a derive-it fallback
  • Trade-a-fact oral rehearsal
