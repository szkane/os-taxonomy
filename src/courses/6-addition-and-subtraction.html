🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍎What the equals sign means — Understand the meaning of the equal sign as 'is the same as' and determine if equations are true or false
  • 🍎Addition and subtraction within 20 — Add and subtract within 20 using strategies such as making ten, decomposing a number leading to ten, and using known facts
  • 🍎Fluent adding and subtracting within 10 — Fluently add and subtract within 10
  • 🍎Adding within 100 — Add within 100 using strategies based on place value, including adding a two-digit and one-digit number, and a two-digit and a multiple of 10
  • 🍎Addition in any order — Understand and apply the commutative property of addition: addends can be added in any order
  • 🍎Estimating by rounding — Estimate the answer to a calculation and use inverse operations to check answers; apply to increasingly large numbers using rounding and inverse reasoning
  • 🍎Finding a missing number in addition — Understand subtraction as finding an unknown addend (e.g. 10 − 8 = ? is the same as 8 + ? = 10)
  • 🍎Inverse: addition undoes subtraction — Recognise and use the inverse relationship between addition and subtraction to check calculations and solve missing-number problems
  • 🍎Mental and written addition and subtraction — Solve addition and subtraction problems using mental and written methods, including problems involving numbers, quantities, and measures
  • 🍎Unknown in Addition & Subtraction — Determine the unknown whole number in an addition or subtraction equation relating three whole numbers
  • 🍎Adding two two-digit numbers — Add and subtract two two-digit numbers using concrete objects, pictorial representations, and mental methods
  • 🍎Fluent addition and subtraction — Recall and use addition and subtraction facts to 20 fluently, and derive and use related facts up to 100
  • 🍎Mental addition and subtraction (age 6+) — Add and subtract a two-digit number and ones mentally and using concrete/pictorial representations
  • 🍎Three-number word problems — Solve word problems that call for adding three whole numbers with a total of 20 or less, representing the problem with objects, a drawing or a three-part bar model and …
  • 🍎Adding and subtracting tens mentally — Add and subtract a two-digit number and tens mentally and using concrete/pictorial representations
  • 🍎Subtracting multiples of 10 — Subtract multiples of 10 (10–90) from multiples of 10 using place value strategies
  • 🍎Adding Three Small Numbers — Add three one-digit numbers using strategies including looking for pairs that make 10
  • 🍎Grouping numbers to add — Understand and apply the associative property of addition: when adding three numbers, any two can be added first
  • 🍎Addition and subtraction strategies — Use counting on and counting back as strategies for addition and subtraction
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is building strong foundations in addition and subtraction — learning to add and subtract numbers fluently within 20, understanding the relationship between these operations, and applying various mental strategies to solve problems.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

What the equals sign means'= means work out the answer, and the answer goes after it' — so 8 = 8 looks unfinished or wrong.Next step: Read '=' aloud as 'is the same value as'; use a pan balance and present answer-left and identity forms until they stop expecting an answer to follow.
Addition and subtraction within 20Partitions the wrong amount when bridging — e.g. 8 + 6: takes 2 to make ten but then adds 6 again, or miscombines 8 + 2 + 4.Next step: Use a ten-frame to see exactly how many spill past ten; rehearse partitioning the second addend (6 = 2 + 4).
Fluent adding and subtracting within 10Counts on from the SMALLER addend — does not use commutativity, so 2 + 7 is counted 3,4,5,6,7,8,9.Next step: Teach 'start from the bigger number' (commutativity) — this is an efficiency habit, not a missing fact.
Adding within 100Treats a multiple of ten as ones — 'thirty-eight plus forty is forty-two' (adds the 4, not 40)Next step: Rebuild with ten-rods: forty is four tens, and it joins the three tens already there. Confront the size mismatch — 42 is barely more than 38.
Addition in any order'You can do any sum in any order' — over-applies commutativity to subtraction, claiming 5 − 8 = 8 − 5 = 3.Next step: Model 8−5 and 5−8 with counters — you cannot take 8 from a pile of 5; order matters. Name addition as the only one of the two that is commutative.
Estimating by rounding'You can't know until you do the real sum' — estimation dismissed as guessing with no valueNext step: In discussion, surface that a rounded ballpark is enough to catch a wildly wrong answer; play 'sensible or silly?' so estimation demonstrably earns its keep. Do not score — draw out the reasoning.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Set 5 + 2 on one pan of a balance and 4 + 3 on the other. Predict whether it will tip or stay level, and say why. Then release it and explain what the balance shows about the '=' sign.

Activity ideas

  • Balance beam: build both sides and watch it level
  • True or false across varied forms
  • Static balance picture to anchor 'the same as'
  • Talk it through: why is this true?
