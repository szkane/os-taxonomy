🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🔋Speed and energy — Use evidence to construct an explanation relating the speed of an object to the energy of that object
  • 🔋Circuit vocabulary — Use technical vocabulary for electrical circuits — circuit, component, cell, battery, current, voltage, resistance, conductor, insulator, switch, series circuit, …
  • 🔋Building an energy-converting device — Apply scientific ideas to design, test, and refine a device that converts energy from one form to another
  • 🔋What happens when things collide — Ask questions and predict outcomes about the changes in energy that occur when objects collide
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring how energy works — understanding how it moves between objects, changes from one form to another, and can be transferred through sound, light, heat, and electricity.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Speed and energyThe fast marble has more force/push, not more energy ('it hits harder because it's got more force').Next step: Separate the words — force is the push during contact; energy is what the moving marble carries and hands over; more speed means more energy to give.
Circuit vocabularyUses 'current' and 'voltage' interchangeably ('the voltage flows through the wire', 'the current of the battery is 3').Next step: Bind each word to its role in a sentence frame — 'the cell's VOLTAGE pushes a CURRENT that FLOWS'; voltage is the push, current is the flow.
Building an energy-converting deviceDevice 'makes' energy: describes the device as creating movement/light out of nothing, omitting the input store (the battery, the winding, the push).Next step: Ask what went IN to make the output happen; trace the chain back to the store (chemical energy in the battery, elastic energy you put in) — nothing is created.
What happens when things collideWhen both objects stop, the energy has just disappeared ('they cancelled out and the energy's gone').Next step: Listen for the sound and feel for warmth; the energy left the kinetic store into sound and thermal — stopping isn't destroying.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Roll the same object down a ramp from a low start, then from a high start (a higher start means it's moving faster at the bottom), into a row of blocks. BEFORE the second roll ask the child to predict how many blocks will move compared with the slow roll, AND why. …

Activity ideas

  • Launch-and-hit bench: build the speed→energy explanation from your own trials
  • Swap the evidence: same rule, different effect
  • Predict-and-check: ramp roll into dominoes (diagnostic reasoning)
  • Name it and reference it: everyday anchors
