🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🔎How data builds explanations — Examine how people use relevant data and observations to develop and check scientific explanations, recognising that data showing a change (e.g. …
  • 🔎Annotated design drawings — Generate and communicate design ideas and decisions using labelled and annotated drawings with technical terms, acknowledging where ideas came from (attribution), and …
  • 🔎Correlation vs Causation — Two things happening together doesn't mean one caused the other — recognise the difference between correlation and causation before drawing conclusions
  • 🔎Comparing Possible Solutions — Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to a problem based on how well each is likely to meet the criteria and constraints
  • 🔎Fair testing (age 8+) — Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are controlled and failure points are considered to identify aspects of a model or prototype that can be improved
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning to think like an engineer — designing solutions to problems, planning fair tests, and improving their ideas based on what works best.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Correlation vs CausationTwo things happening together means one causes the other ('they go up together, so ice cream causes sunburn').Next step: Introduce the idea of a third thing behind both; ask 'what happens on those days that could cause BOTH?'
Comparing Possible SolutionsFixates on the first idea and elaborates it instead of generating real alternatives (design fixation) — 'I'll just make this one better'.Next step: Require three visibly different starting shapes before any judging; praise divergence first, evaluation second.
Fair testing (age 8+)Unfair retest: loads the improved bridge more gently, or in a different spot, so 'it's better' isn't a fair claim.Next step: Hold the loading method identical between runs; only the design should change, so any difference is down to the design.

Activity ideas

  • Bust a fake cause by manipulating a system
  • Name the confounder or the test that settles it
  • Interrogate a bold causal headline
  • Solution Showdown: build and reason across your own comparison matrix
