🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🔎Asking scientific questions — Ask simple scientific questions and recognise that they can be answered in different ways including observation, testing, and research
  • 🔎Observing with simple equipment — Observe closely using simple equipment such as hand lenses, and use observations to describe, compare, and identify things
  • 🔎Simple tests and experiments — Perform simple tests and use observations and ideas to suggest answers to questions
  • 🔎Did it happen as predicted? — With guidance, compare what was observed in a simple investigation with the prediction made beforehand, and say whether the prediction matched

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Asking scientific questionsEvery question is answered by asking a grown-up or looking in a book ('I'd ask my teacher', 'I'd look it up') — treats knowledge as coming from authority rather than from finding out.Next step: Accept that books and experts are real sources, then push: 'What if no book knows about THESE woodlice in THIS garden? How could you find out yourself?' Model turning a wonder into a thing you do.
Observing with simple equipmentReports from memory, not observation: describes what leaves are 'supposed' to look like without looking closely through the lens.Next step: 'Look through the lens — what can you see now that you couldn't before?' Anchor description to the specimen.
Simple tests and experimentsUncontrolled comparison: gives the car a harder push, or a different starting height, on the surface they want to win — 'I helped it go on the wood'.Next step: Ask 'did each surface get a fair go? what did you change?' Model releasing (not pushing) from the same spot each time.

Activity ideas

  • Question Workbench: sort each wonder into how you'd answer it
  • Sharpen a floppy wonder into an answerable question
  • Which questions can science settle, and which can't?
  • Wonder together with a curious scientist (spoken dialogue)
