🎯 What you’ll learn

  • ⬡Turns & Directions — Describe movement and direction, including whole, half, quarter, and three-quarter turns
  • ⬡Patterns & Sequences — Order and arrange combinations of mathematical objects in patterns and sequences
  • ⬡Building & Drawing Shapes — Model shapes by building from components (e.g. sticks and clay balls) and by drawing
  • ⬡Flat vs Solid Shapes — Distinguish two-dimensional (flat) shapes from three-dimensional (solid) shapes
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring shapes and space — recognising circles, triangles, and squares, building with 3D shapes like cubes and spheres, and describing where things are using words like above, below, and beside.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Turns & Directions'Turns' by walking forward in a curve or stepping sideways instead of rotating on the spotNext step: Mark the spot with a hoop or footprints and require both feet to stay put, turning to face a named target.
Patterns & SequencesExtends by visually matching and appending the last item, without grasping there is a repeating unitNext step: Label the model 'same, same, different' and box the repeating chunk; rebuild from that unit before extending
Building & Drawing ShapesUses the wrong number of components — grabs four sticks for a triangle, or the wrong count of straws/connectors for a cube — not matching parts to the shape's attributes.Next step: Name the attributes first ('a triangle has 3 sides and 3 corners → 3 sticks, 3 balls') and count the parts before building; compare the finished model against a real shape.
Flat vs Solid ShapesApplies a flat (2-D) name to a solid object — shown a real ball or cube, calls it 'a circle' or 'a square' and sorts it with the flat shapesNext step: Hand the child the object to hold and turn: 'a circle is flat on the paper; the ball is round every way and you can pick it up — that is a sphere'; teach the twin-pair names

Activity ideas

  • Turn your whole body on the spot
  • Give and follow a route
  • Program a robot along a floor grid (companion)
  • Build, extend, and name the unit of repeat
