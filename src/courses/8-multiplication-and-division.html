🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🥚What Multiplication Means — Interpret products of whole numbers (e.g. 5 × 7 as the total number of objects in 5 groups of 7)
  • 🥚All times tables to 12×12 — Recall multiplication and division facts for multiplication tables up to 12 × 12
  • 🥚Written Multiplication — Multiply two-digit and three-digit numbers by a one-digit number using formal written layout
  • 🥚Fluent multiplication and division facts — Fluently multiply and divide within 100 using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division
  • 🥚Division as Unknown Factor — Understand division as an unknown-factor problem (e.g. find 32 ÷ 8 by finding the number that makes 32 when multiplied by 8)
  • 🥚Properties of Operations — Apply properties of operations (commutative, associative, distributive) as strategies to multiply and divide
  • 🥚What Division Means — Interpret whole-number quotients (e.g. 56 ÷ 8 as the number of objects in each share or the number of equal groups)
  • 🥚Factor Pairs & Commutativity — Recognise and use factor pairs and commutativity in mental calculations
  • 🥚Multiply & Add Problems — Solve problems involving multiplying and adding, including using the distributive law, integer scaling problems, and harder correspondence problems
  • 🥚Patterns in Times Tables — Identify arithmetic patterns (including patterns in the addition table or multiplication table) and explain them using properties of operations
  • 🥚Multiplying by Tens — Multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10–90 using strategies based on place value and properties of operations
  • 🥚Mental multiplication and division — Use place value, known and derived facts to multiply and divide mentally, including multiplying by 0 and 1, dividing by 1, and multiplying together three numbers
  • 🥚Multiplication and Division Word Problems — Use multiplication and division within 100 to solve word problems involving equal groups, arrays, and measurement quantities
  • 🥚Unknown in Multiplication & Division — Determine the unknown whole number in a multiplication or division equation relating three whole numbers (e.g. 8 × ? = 48, ? × 6 = 42)
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is building fluency with multiplication and division — memorizing times tables up to 12×12, understanding the relationship between multiplication and division, and solving word problems involving equal groups and arrays.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

What Multiplication Meansadds the two numbers — reads a multiplication story as 'combine the numbers I see' (4 + 6 = 10) rather than four groups of sixNext step: build the four groups of six with counters or an array, count the total, and set it beside 4 + 6 so the difference between joining two numbers and making equal groups is visible
All times tables to 12×12no derivation backup — when a fact isn't recalled, guesses rather than building from a known neighbourNext step: teach 'if you're stuck, build from a fact you know' (7 × 8 = 7 × 7 + 7) so derivation is the safety net
Written Multiplicationadds the carry BEFORE multiplying the next digit — e.g. for 47×6 does (4+4)×6 = 48 instead of 4×6 then +4Next step: slow the step: multiply the digit first, say the product, THEN add the little carried number; expanded/grid method briefly to show the carry is a separate addition.
Fluent multiplication and division factsno derived-fact strategy — when a fact isn't instantly known, counts up rather than deriving from a near fact (9 × 7 via 10 × 7 − 7)Next step: explicitly teach near-fact strategies (×9 via ×10 then subtract; ×6 via ×5 then add) and rehearse them until they are reached for first
Division as Unknown Factorfinds the answer only by dealing out / repeated subtraction and cannot connect it to a multiplication fact — sees ÷ and × as unrelated proceduresNext step: lay out the same 32 in a 8×4 array; show the ONE array answers both 8×4=? and 32÷8=?; build a fact-family triangle so the inverse link is explicit.
Properties of Operationstreats distributive split additively on both parts: '8×7 = 8×5 + 2 = 42' — adds the leftover 2 instead of 8×2Next step: draw the array: 8 rows of 7, cut it into 8 rows of 5 and 8 rows of 2; ask how many squares are in the second block — it is 8×2=16, not 2. Make the split visible before returning to symbols.
