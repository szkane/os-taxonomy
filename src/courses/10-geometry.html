🎯 What you’ll learn

  • ⬡Coordinates (age 10+) — Describe positions on the full coordinate grid (all four quadrants); use coordinates with negative values
  • ⬡Translating and reflecting shapes — Draw and translate simple shapes on the coordinate plane; reflect shapes in the axes
  • ⬡Classifying shapes by properties — Compare and classify geometric shapes based on their properties and sizes; understand that attributes belonging to a category also belong to all subcategories; classify …
  • ⬡Angles in triangles (age 10+) — Find unknown angles in triangles, quadrilaterals, and regular polygons using angle sum properties
  • ⬡Plotting points in the first quadrant — Plot and read ordered pairs in the first quadrant of the coordinate plane; represent real-world and mathematical problems by graphing points and interpreting coordinate …
  • ⬡2-D shapes (age 10+) — Draw 2-D shapes using given dimensions and angles, using a ruler and protractor accurately
  • ⬡The coordinate grid — Understand a coordinate system defined by two perpendicular number lines (axes) with an origin at (0,0); know that an ordered pair (x, y) specifies a unique point where …
  • ⬡Understanding angles (age 10+) — Recognise angles where they meet at a point, are on a straight line, or are vertically opposite; find missing angles using these properties
  • ⬡3-D shapes (age 10+) — Recognise, describe, and build simple 3-D shapes, including making nets
  • ⬡Parts of a circle — Illustrate and name parts of circles, including radius, diameter, and circumference; know that the diameter is twice the radius
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is developing advanced spatial skills — working with 3D shapes and nets, using coordinate grids with negative numbers, calculating angles, and understanding geometric transformations like reflection and translation.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Coordinates (age 10+)Moves the wrong way for a negative coordinate — counts right for −3 or up for −5 — treating the minus as decoration rather than a directionNext step: Rehearse the axis as a signed number line with a counting stick — negatives go left/down from 0, symmetric about the origin; re-plot reading the sign as the direction.
Translating and reflecting shapesReflects in the x-axis but changes the sign of the wrong coordinate — negates x instead of y — effectively reflecting in the other axisNext step: Anchor to the geometry: reflecting in the x-axis flips the shape vertically, so the vertical (y) coordinate changes; move each vertex perpendicular to the axis and read off (x, −y). Contrast with a y-axis reflection on the same shape.
Classifying shapes by propertiesReverses the subset direction — picks 'all rectangles are squares' as the true one (treats the more familiar/larger category as the special case)Next step: Use nested-set and property-table work to show which set contains which: the square set sits INSIDE the rectangle set, so going square→rectangle always holds but rectangle→square does not. Revisit classifying by parallel/perpendicular-line properties.
Angles in triangles (age 10+)Thinks a triangle's angles sum to 360° — subtracts the two known angles from 360 instead of 180.Next step: Re-derive 180° by tearing the corners onto a straight line; contrast triangle (180°) with quadrilateral (360°) and show the quadrilateral is TWO triangles.
Plotting points in the first quadrantReverses the pair — goes up before across, plotting (4,7) where (7,4) belongs ('you go up 4 and along 7')Next step: Reteach the fixed order as a movement rule — 'along the corridor before up the stairs', x always first — and have them narrate the route aloud as they plot
2-D shapes (age 10+)Reads the wrong scale on the dual-scale protractor, drawing the supplement of the intended angleNext step: Install the estimate-first rule: 'is this bigger or smaller than a square corner?' then choose the scale whose number agrees; rehearse on a single-scale/numberless protractor until scale choice is automatic
