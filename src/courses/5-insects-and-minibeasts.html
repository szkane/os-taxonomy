Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

What is a minibeast?Minibeasts aren't animals — 'only things like dogs and lions are animals.'Next step: Widen the category: animals include tiny, no-backbone creatures; show minibeasts move, eat and grow like any animal.
Common minibeasts: naming and recognisingThey're all just 'bugs' — 'a spider is an insect too, same as a ladybird.'Next step: Contrast a countable feature — the spider's eight legs versus the ladybird's six — so the child sees they're different kinds.
Minibeast HabitatsThe same minibeasts live everywhere — 'you'd find the same ones in both places.'Next step: Pair each creature to its conditions — damp/dark for woodlice, sunny leaves for aphids — so place predicts creature.
Minibeasts in the food chainThe arrow points to what the animal eats — so 'caterpillar → bird' means the caterpillar eats the bird.Next step: Re-read the arrow as 'is eaten by' / 'energy flows to' — it points toward the animal that gets the meal.
How minibeasts moveThinks every minibeast has the same number of legs — gives the spider 6 like the ant, or says they all have 'lots of legs' ('they're all bugs, so they're all the same')Next step: Put a real (or large photo of an) ant and spider side by side and count together, touching each leg. Name the difference: 6 legs = insect, 8 legs = not an insect (spider). Let the count, not the guess, settle it.
Caterpillar to butterflyBelieves the caterpillar and butterfly are two separate animals — 'the caterpillar dies and the butterfly is a different creature'Next step: Follow one named caterpillar through photos day by day into and out of the chrysalis. Emphasise it is the SAME individual the whole time — draw arrows connecting the stages as one creature's story, not four animals.
