🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧱Place value of each digit — Recognise the place value of each digit in a four-digit number (thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones)
  • 🧱Negative Numbers — Count backwards through zero to include negative numbers
  • 🧱Reading and writing numbers (age 9+) — Read, write, order, and compare whole numbers up to at least 1,000,000 using base-ten numerals, number names, expanded form, and place-value understanding
  • 🧱Comparing Large Numbers — Order and compare numbers beyond 1000
  • 🧱Rounding to 10, 100, 1000 — Round any number to the nearest 10, 100, or 1000
  • 🧱Numbers to 10,000 — Identify, represent, and estimate numbers up to 10,000 using different representations
  • 🧱Place Value Problem-Solving — Solve number and practical problems involving place value with increasingly large positive numbers
  • 🧱Roman numerals to 100 — Read Roman numerals to 100 (I to C) and understand that the numeral system changed over time to include zero and place value
  • 🧱1000 More or Less — Find 1000 more or less than a given number
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is working with larger numbers up to 10,000 — understanding place value in four-digit numbers, learning to round numbers, and exploring negative numbers and Roman numerals.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Place value of each digitFace value — 'the 4 is just 4', the same in both numbers, ignoring its column.Next step: Partition 3,452 with base-ten blocks or a place-value chart; show the 4 as four hundred-flats, then move it to the tens column to see its worth become 40.
Negative NumbersOrders negatives by magnitude — '−5 is bigger than −2 because 5 is bigger than 2'.Next step: Walk both on a horizontal number line; show −5 sits further left, and 'smaller' means further left, so −5 < −2 even though 5 > 2.
Reading and writing numbers (age 9+)Reads/writes ignoring placeholder zeros or period grouping: 403,072 read as 'forty-three thousand...', or 'four hundred thousand and seven' written 400,700 / 4,000,007Next step: Group digits into three-digit periods (thousands | ones) and read period by period; use a place-value chart so each zero holds its column.
Comparing Large NumbersCompares digit-by-digit from the right, or by which number has a bigger digit somewhere, instead of from the highest placeNext step: Right-align the numbers in place-value columns and compare from the leftmost place, stopping at the first place that differs.
Rounding to 10, 100, 1000Decides using the wrong digit — the hundreds digit itself, or the final (ones) digit — instead of the digit one place to the right of the rounding placeNext step: Name the 'decider' digit: the one immediately to the right of the place you're rounding to. Mark it, then look only at that. Reground with place-value columns.
Numbers to 10,000Drops the empty hundreds column or concatenates the counts — writes 342, or writes 3,42 / 30042, because the zero 'has nothing so it doesn't count'.Next step: Return to the columns: show that removing the zero slides the 3 out of the thousands place and changes its value; the zero holds the hundreds place open.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Set a model thermometer to 3°C. Before moving anything, ask the child to predict the reading after it drops 5 degrees and to say why. A child who thinks numbers stop at zero predicts 0 (or 'it can't go lower'); a child who understands negatives predicts −2. …
