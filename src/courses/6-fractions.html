📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is beginning to understand fractions by dividing shapes and objects into equal parts, learning to recognize and write simple fractions like halves, quarters, and thirds.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Fractions of amountsReads the denominator as the group SIZE, not the number of groups - for 1/3 of 12 makes groups of 3Next step: Emphasise the 3 means THREE equal groups; share all 12 into three hoops and take one.
Decomposing a shape into more equal sharesLarger denominator means larger fraction - 'a quarter is bigger because 4 is more than 2' (whole-number bias)Next step: Predict-then-overlay two identical wholes (see poeTask); name the pattern 'more shares = smaller piece'.
Halves & Quarters of ShapesThe right number of pieces means the fraction - accepts unequal parts as halves/quartersNext step: Overlay/rotate the parts to test equality; contrast an equal and an unequal partition of identical shapes.
Fraction NotationReads the symbol as two separate whole numbers — '3/8' becomes 'three, eight' rather than one quantity, three-eighthsNext step: Locate the fraction as one point on a number line and say it as one word ('three-eighths'); repeatedly read symbol→word→shaded amount as a single quantity
Understanding fractions2/4 is more than 1/2 - 'two pieces is more than one', or 'the numbers are bigger' (whole-number bias)Next step: Predict-then-overlay two quarters onto one half (see poeTask); they coincide, so the amounts are equal.
Comparing fractionsWhole-number bias on unit fractions — orders 1/5 > 1/4 > 1/3 > 1/2, treating the larger denominator as the larger fractionNext step: Lay the unit fractions as aligned bars against one whole so 1/5 is visibly smallest; cut a whole into more pieces to feel each shrink; reteach more-parts-means-smaller
