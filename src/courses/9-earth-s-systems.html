🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🌍Salt Water vs Fresh Water — Describe and graph the amounts of salt water and fresh water in various reservoirs to provide evidence about the distribution of water on Earth
  • 🌍Erosion and weathering — Make observations and measurements to provide evidence of the effects of weathering or the rate of erosion by water, ice, wind, or vegetation
  • 🌍Types of rocks — Use vocabulary for Earth's geological processes and rock types — igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic, erosion, weathering, deposition, fossil, sediment, strata, permeable, …
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning how Earth's surface changes over time — studying how wind, water, and ice shape our landscape through weathering and erosion, and interpreting maps to understand geological patterns.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Salt Water vs Fresh WaterMost of Earth's water is fresh / drinkable ('there's water everywhere, so most is drinkable')Next step: Confront with the ~97/3 split; graph it, e.g. 97 of 100 cups salty.
Erosion and weatheringWeathering and erosion are the same thing — uses them interchangeably ('the rock eroded away' for breaking in place)Next step: Confront it — weathering breaks the rock where it sits, erosion carries the pieces away; demonstrate each step on its own.
Types of rocksSwaps weathering and erosion ('the rock eroded into bits' for breaking; 'it weathered downstream' for moving)Next step: Confront it — weathering breaks, erosion carries; have them re-tell with each word in its correct slot.

Activity ideas

  • Build a reservoir graph, then zoom into the fresh-water slice
  • Hands-on beaker and 100-drops demonstration
  • Read and reason from a supplied chart
  • Motivating scaled-volume hook
