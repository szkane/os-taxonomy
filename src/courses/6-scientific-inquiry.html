Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Observation vs InterpretationTreats interpretation as observation: says 'the sun dried it up' is something you saw.Next step: 'Did you SEE the sun do it, or did you see the puddle get smaller and then guess why?'
Drawing conclusions from evidenceConclusion from prior belief, not data: agrees 'paper towel is best' because that's what they use at home, even though it soaked up the least.Next step: Point back to the data — which number is biggest? — and rebuild the conclusion from the evidence.
Gathering, recording, classifying and presenting dataTable is disorganised — no clear headings, or categories mixed together — so it doesn't show the data clearly.Next step: Decide the columns and their headings before entering data; one kind of information per column.
Changing Your Mind with EvidenceDiscounts the surprising result as a mistake or a fluke to protect the prediction ('I must have done it wrong', 'the marble is broken').Next step: Repeat the observation together so it can't be dismissed; then ask 'if it keeps happening, what does that tell us about our idea?'

Activity ideas

  • Two Buckets: sort what I saw from what I think
  • Snip the 'because' tail: rewrite to a pure observation
  • Science-buddy dialogue: 'What did you SEE? What do you THINK it means?'
  • Lift-the-flap scene: observation first, idea underneath
