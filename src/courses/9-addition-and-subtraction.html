🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍎Fluent addition and subtraction (age 9+) — Fluently add and subtract whole numbers with more than four digits using the standard columnar algorithm
  • 🍎Adding and subtracting (age 9+) — Solve addition and subtraction multi-step problems in contexts, deciding which operations and methods to use and why
  • 🍎Checking Answers by Rounding — Use rounding to check answers to calculations and determine appropriate levels of accuracy in context
  • 🍎Mental addition and subtraction (age 9+) — Add and subtract numbers mentally with increasingly large numbers, using place-value knowledge and derived facts
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering mental arithmetic with large numbers and using formal written methods for complex calculations, while learning to choose the most efficient method for different types of problems.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Fluent addition and subtraction (age 9+)Cannot cascade an exchange through several zeros in 500,000 - 234,178 - stalls, or borrows only one column and leaves the rest as 0/9 wronglyNext step: Model with place-value counters: exchange one hundred-thousand and walk it down column by column so each 0 becomes 9 with a ten passed on.
Adding and subtracting (age 9+)Gives the running total (20,822) as the answer, forgetting the question asked how many MORE are neededNext step: Re-read and underline the actual question; mark all three steps on the bar so the final subtraction is not dropped.
Checking Answers by RoundingThinks a good estimate should equal the exact answer, so calls 71,000 'wrong' because the real answer is 70,988Next step: Discuss: an estimate is a deliberate ballpark, not a rival answer; its job is to catch big errors, and being near (not equal) is exactly right.
Mental addition and subtraction (age 9+)Misplaces the zeros when scaling a bond - '45,000 + 8,000' given as 125,000, 5,300, or 53,00Next step: Slide the fact up the place-value chart (45 + 8 = 53, so 45 thousand + 8 thousand = 53 thousand); make the unit explicit.

Activity ideas

  • Borrowing across a row of zeros (500,000 − 234,178)
  • Adding three numbers of up to five digits: when the carry is more than one
  • Worked-example reference and estimate-then-check routine
  • Plan-then-compute with a multi-bar model
