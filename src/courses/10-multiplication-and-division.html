🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🥚Long multiplication (age 10+) — Fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers (up to 4 digits by 2 digits) using the formal written method of long multiplication
  • 🥚Division with remainders (age 10+) — Divide numbers up to 4 digits by a two-digit divisor using formal written long division, interpreting remainders as whole numbers, fractions, or by rounding as …
  • 🥚Rounding Answers — Solve problems which require answers to be rounded to specified degrees of accuracy
  • 🥚Brackets in Expressions — Use parentheses, brackets, or braces in numerical expressions and evaluate expressions containing these grouping symbols
  • 🥚Order of operations — Understand and apply the conventional order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS) to carry out calculations involving the four operations
  • 🥚Division with Decimals — Use written division methods in cases where the answer has up to two decimal places; divide decimals to hundredths by whole numbers
  • 🥚Writing Number Sentences — Write simple numerical expressions that record calculations with numbers, and interpret numerical expressions without evaluating them (e.g. …
  • 🥚Multiplying and dividing (age 10+) — Multiply and divide numbers by 10, 100, and 1000 giving answers up to three decimal places, understanding that digits shift position in the place-value chart
  • 🥚Decimal place value — Multiply one-digit numbers with up to two decimal places by whole numbers (e.g. 3.47 × 6)
  • 🥚Multi-step problems: choosing operations — Solve problems involving addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, deciding which operations and methods to use and why; solve multi-step problems in contexts
  • 🥚Estimation to check answers to calculations — Use estimation to check answers to calculations and determine, in the context of a problem, an appropriate degree of accuracy
  • 🥚Mental calculation strategies — Perform mental calculations including with mixed operations and large numbers, using strategies such as partitioning, compensation, and derived facts
  • 🥚Dividing by two-digit numbers — Divide numbers up to 4 digits by a two-digit number using formal written short division where appropriate, interpreting remainders according to context

📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is advancing to sophisticated multiplication and division — using formal written methods for complex calculations, working with decimals, and applying the order of operations to solve multi-step problems.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Long multiplication (age 10+)omits or misplaces the placeholder zero on the tens-digit rowNext step: reinforce that the second row multiplies by 3 TENS, so it must start in the tens column — write the zero to hold the ones place; connect to 'thirty groups'.
Division with remainders (age 10+)misplaces quotient digits / omits a zero in the quotient when 13 doesn't go into a brought-down valueNext step: insist that every brought-down digit produces a quotient digit — write a 0 when the divisor doesn't fit; align each quotient digit above the digit being divided.
Rounding Answersapplies 'round to nearest' mechanically in the coaches context — 5.2 rounds to 5, stranding peopleNext step: ask what happens to the extra 10 people; a real 'how many needed' context always rounds UP to fit everyone, regardless of the decimal.
Brackets in Expressionsignores the brackets and evaluates left to right — 3 × (4+5) done as 3×4 then +5 = 17Next step: name brackets as 'do me first' — the grouping is a wrapper that must be collapsed to a single number before anything outside it; box the bracket and replace it with its value.
Order of operationsevaluates strictly left to right, ignoring operation priority — 8 + 2 = 10, × 5 = 50Next step: show the expression as 8 plus a group '2 × 5'; the multiplication binds its numbers together first. Contrast 8 + 2×5 with (8+2)×5 to show the convention matters.
Division with Decimalsmisplaces or ignores the decimal point in the quotient — divides the digits as if whole, giving 369 or 36.9Next step: line the quotient's decimal point directly above the dividend's; reason about magnitude (14.76÷4 is about 3.7, so 3.69 not 369).
