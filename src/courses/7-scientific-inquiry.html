🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🔎Using evidence to answer questions — Identify differences, similarities, or changes related to scientific ideas and use straightforward scientific evidence to answer questions or support findings
  • 🔎Could there be another explanation? — For any result, ask: is there another explanation? — the first explanation that fits isn't always the right one, and good scientists actively look for alternatives
  • 🔎Measuring accurately — Make systematic and careful observations, take accurate measurements using standard units and equipment including thermometers and data loggers
  • 🔎Simple Design Problems — Define a simple design problem reflecting a need or want that includes specified criteria for success and constraints on materials, time, or cost
  • 🔎Fair testing — Set up simple practical enquiries, comparative tests, and fair tests, understanding the importance of changing only one variable at a time
  • 🔎Judging designs against criteria — Evaluate design ideas and finished solutions against criteria — personal preferences or given, co-developed or agreed criteria — including sustainability (e.g. …
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning to think and work like a scientist by setting up fair tests, making careful observations and measurements, recording data in charts and tables, and drawing conclusions from their investigations.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Using evidence to answer questionsAnswers from prior belief, ignores the data: says minibeasts like flowers or gardens regardless of the chart.Next step: Point back to the specific numbers as the evidence that must support the answer.
Could there be another explanation?Accepts the first explanation that fits and stops searching ('yes, it must be the talking').Next step: Prompt 'what else was different between the windowsill and the cupboard?'; make listing alternatives the habit before choosing one.
Measuring accuratelyRuler zero-error: measures from the end of the ruler or from the '1' mark instead of 0.Next step: Line the object up with the 0 mark; the gap before 0 isn't part of the length.
Simple Design ProblemsJumps straight to a solution instead of defining the problem ('we should just get blinds').Next step: Bracket the solution: 'That might be one answer — but first, what does ANY good answer have to achieve?' Redirect to problem and criteria before solutions.
Fair testingConfounded test: changes more than one variable — makes the big-wing plane from thicker paper too, or throws it harder — so any difference can't be pinned on the wings.Next step: Ask 'which of your two planes is different in more than one way?' Rebuild so only the wings change.
