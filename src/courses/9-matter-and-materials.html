🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧊Dissolving & Solutions — Understand that some materials dissolve in liquid to form a solution, and describe how to recover a substance from a solution by evaporation
  • 🧊Irreversible Changes — Explain that some changes result in the formation of new materials and are not usually reversible, such as burning, rusting, and reactions with acid
  • 🧊Reversible Changes — Demonstrate that dissolving, mixing, and changes of state are reversible changes where no new materials are formed
  • 🧊Natural and made materials — Classify materials and fibres as natural (e.g. wool, cotton, silk, wood, stone) or made/processed (e.g. …
  • 🧊Separating Mixtures — Use knowledge of solids, liquids, and gases to decide how mixtures might be separated through filtering, sieving, and evaporating
  • 🧊Advanced Material Properties — Compare and group everyday materials based on advanced properties: hardness, solubility, transparency, electrical and thermal conductivity, and response to magnets
  • 🧊Material Properties Vocabulary — Use technical vocabulary to describe and compare material properties — conductor, insulator, thermal, electrical, transparent, opaque, translucent, soluble, insoluble, …
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring how materials behave and change — investigating which substances dissolve in water, learning to separate mixtures, and distinguishing between changes that can be reversed and those that create entirely new materials.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Dissolving & Solutionsthe dissolved solid has been destroyed ('it turned into water' / 'it's gone')Next step: Evaporate the solution together so the salt reappears; weigh before and after to show mass is conserved. This is the Piagetian conservation gap — show, don't just assert.
Irreversible Changesthe reaction just mixed or hid the ingredients, which could be un-mixed ('the vinegar's still in there somewhere')Next step: Contrast with a true mixture (sand+salt, separable) versus this (a new gas escaped, new substance made). The escaping gas is the giveaway that it isn't just mixed.
Reversible Changesany change caused by heating is irreversible ('you heated it, so it can't go back')Next step: Line up melting ice (heated, reversible) against toasting (heated, irreversible) — force the child off 'heat' and onto 'was a new substance made?'.
Separating Mixturesevaporates first, before filtering ('boil off the water, then I've got the solids')Next step: Don't just correct the order — ask 'which of these is dissolved and which is just floating?'; rebuild from the property (insoluble vs dissolved), then let them re-plan.
Advanced Material Properties'All metals are magnetic' — magnetism treated as a defining property of metal.Next step: Test aluminium, copper and brass with a magnet — no attraction — and name the few magnetic metals (iron/steel, nickel, cobalt).
Material Properties Vocabulary'Translucent' collapsed into transparent or opaque — the missing-middle term is skipped; frosted glass is called 'transparent' or 'a bit opaque'.Next step: Run a three-way torch test (clear film / frosted glass / card) and have the child name each with the matching word.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Predict-Observe-Explain: weigh a glass of water on a balance, note the reading, then ask the child to predict what the balance shows AFTER a spoon of salt is stirred in until invisible — and crucially WHY. A child who thinks dissolving destroys matter predicts the same or a lower reading. …

Activity ideas

  • Dissolve, weigh, recover bench
  • Zoom-in: where did the sugar go?
  • Predict-Observe-Explain and check your reasoning
  • New-substance detector bench with failed reversals
