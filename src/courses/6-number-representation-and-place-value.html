🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧱A Ten Is Ten Ones — Understand that 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones — called a 'ten'
  • 🧱The two digits of a two-digit number — Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones
  • 🧱A Hundred Is Ten Tens — Understand that 100 can be thought of as a bundle of ten tens — called a 'hundred'
  • 🧱Comparing and ordering numbers — Compare and order two-digit numbers, and numbers across 100 up to 120, using the symbols >, =, and <, based on place value understanding
  • 🧱Renaming two-digit numbers — Partition and rename two-digit numbers in standard and non-standard ways (e.g. …
  • 🧱Representing numbers to 120 — Identify, represent and estimate numbers to 120 using different representations: place a number on a number line marked in tens, represent it with base-ten blocks or …
  • 🧱10 More or 10 Less — Mentally find 10 more or 10 less than a given two-digit number without counting
  • 🧱Number Words to 100 — Read and write numbers to at least 100 in words
  • 🧱Place value understanding and number facts — Use place value understanding and number facts to solve problems
  • 🧱The multiples of 10 — Understand that the multiples of 10 (10, 20, 30 … 90) represent one to nine tens and 0 ones
  • 🧱Reading and writing numbers to 120 — Count to 120 starting at any number less than 120; read and write numerals to 120
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning how numbers work by understanding place value — discovering that in two-digit numbers, each digit has a different value depending on whether it represents tens or ones.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

A Ten Is Ten OnesTreats the ten-stick as a single unit worth one, so the loose cubes look like more: 'there are ten over here but the stick is just one'Next step: Break the stick apart in front of them and count 10; rebuild it. Name it: '10 ones make 1 ten — same cubes, bundled up.' Use exchange games (10 ones ⇄ 1 ten).
The two digits of a two-digit numberReads the digit at face value: 'the 4 means four'Next step: Build 47 with 4 tens rods and 7 ones; count the ones inside the rods (40). Say 'the 4 is in the tens place, so it's 4 tens = 40'.
A Hundred Is Ten TensCounts the sticks rather than their value: 'there are ten sticks so it's ten'Next step: Count the ones inside one stick (10), then skip-count the sticks in tens: 10, 20 … 100. Name the new bundle 'one hundred = ten tens = a hundred ones'.
Comparing and ordering numbersCompares by a single digit's size regardless of place: thinks 19 > 51 because 9 > 1Next step: Align base-ten representations by hundreds, tens and ones. Compare the highest relevant place first: hundreds across 100, then tens and ones if higher places tie. Re-order with the models in view.
Representing numbers to 120Uses only the endpoints with no interior benchmark, so placement is a rough guess with no sense of the middleNext step: Establish the midpoint first: fold/find 50, then reason 40 is a little to its left. Build the halfway benchmark before fine placement.
10 More or 10 LessChanges the ones digit instead of the tens: '10 more than 56 is 57'Next step: Contrast 'one more' vs 'ten more' on a base-ten model; ten more adds a ten (tens digit +1), the ones stay put.

Activity ideas

  • Base-ten exchange: bundle and break a ten
  • Reveal the ten inside the rod
  • Prove it to the doubter
  • Check the ten-ones equivalence
