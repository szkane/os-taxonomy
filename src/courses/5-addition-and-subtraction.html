🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍎Reading +, −, and = symbols — Read, write, and interpret the symbols +, −, and = in number sentences
  • 🍎Number bonds — Recall number bonds (addition facts and related subtraction facts): within 10 at the first stage (as in Ontario Grade 1), extending to within 20 (as in England Year 1 …
  • 🍎Adding and subtracting — Add and subtract one-digit and two-digit numbers to 20, including zero
  • 🍎Early Word Problems — Solve one-step word problems involving addition and subtraction to 20, including missing-number problems
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is building a strong foundation in addition and subtraction, learning to combine and separate numbers up to 20 using objects, drawings, and mental strategies.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Reading +, −, and = symbolsReads = as 'the answer is' / 'makes the total' (operator view), so cannot cope with the sum on the right or a missing addend.Next step: Use a balance and 'is the same as' language; show true/false equations with the total first (5 = 3 + 2) so = is read relationally.
Number bondsNo bridging strategy — counts on by ones for teen sums (8+5 counted 9,10,11,12,13 on fingers), so it is not a recalled bond.Next step: Teach make-ten (8+2+3) so crossing-ten sums become derived facts; re-secure bonds to 10 as the foundation.
Adding and subtractingAdds/subtracts to the wrong part, or counts all from one instead of counting on from 14.Next step: Use tens-and-ones or a number line — hold 14, count on 5, adding ones to the ones; make the place value visible.
Early Word ProblemsKeyword strategy — picks the operation from a word ('away/left' → subtract) regardless of structure, so start-unknown '5 drive away, 7 left' is computed as 7 − 5 or 5 − 7.Next step: Model the situation with counters or a bar model before choosing an operation; do not teach keyword-to-operation rules.

Activity ideas

  • Sentence builder linked to a balance
  • Both directions: read-and-act, then write-to-match
  • Symbol-and-action reference wall
  • Notation practice: choose and judge
