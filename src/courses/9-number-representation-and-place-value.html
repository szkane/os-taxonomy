🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧱Place Value × 10 Pattern — Recognise that in a multi-digit whole number, a digit in one place represents ten times what it represents in the place to its right (e.g. 700 ÷ 70 = 10)
  • 🧱Negative numbers in context — Interpret negative numbers in context (temperature, sea level, bank balance); count forwards and backwards with positive and negative whole numbers, including through …
  • 🧱Rounding Large Numbers — Round any whole number up to 1,000,000 to the nearest 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, or 100,000 using place-value understanding
  • 🧱Working with Large Numbers — Solve number and practical problems involving reading, writing, ordering, comparing, and rounding whole numbers up to 1,000,000
  • 🧱Roman numerals to 1000 — Read Roman numerals to 1000 (M) and recognise years written in Roman numerals
  • 🧱Counting forwards and backwards (age 9+) — Count forwards and backwards in steps of powers of 10 (10, 100, 1000, 10,000, 100,000) for any given number up to 1,000,000
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is working with larger numbers up to 1 million — understanding place value, rounding to different levels of accuracy, and beginning to work with negative numbers in everyday contexts.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Place Value × 10 PatternCompares the two values by subtracting instead of by a scale factor: '3,000 is 2,700 more than 300'Next step: Re-voice the question as scaling: '300 fits into 3,000 how many times?' Build both with place-value counters and show 300 stacked ten times to reach 3,000.
Negative numbers in contextIgnores the sign and treats −4 as 4: −4 + 7 = 11Next step: Put −4 on a number line to the LEFT of zero; count 7 steps right and see you only reach 3. Contrast with starting at +4.
Rounding Large NumbersUses the wrong column to decide — not the digit immediately to the right of the target placeNext step: Mark the target place and its 'decider' (one to the right); look only at that. Reground with a labelled place-value chart for six-digit numbers.
Working with Large NumbersDoesn't prioritise place value for magnitude — spreads the digit-sum evenly or fills low places, so the number isn't the largest (e.g. 333,300 or 111,138)Next step: Compare two candidates place by place: a 9 in the hundred-thousands beats any arrangement with a smaller leading digit. Make the leading-place-dominates idea explicit.
Roman numerals to 1000Adds all symbols regardless of order: reads IV as 6 (I + V) or MCMXCIX as a running sumNext step: Show that a smaller symbol BEFORE a larger means subtract (IV = 5 − 1); after means add (VI = 6). Group MCMXCIX into subtractive pairs.
Counting forwards and backwards (age 9+)Adjusts the wrong place value — counts on in ten-thousands but changes the thousands or hundred-thousands digit (462,000 → 472,000 becomes 463,000 or 562,000).Next step: Point to the ten-thousands column on a place-value chart and count with only that digit changing; say the count as '46 ten-thousands, 47 ten-thousands...'.

Activity ideas

  • Slide a digit across the place-value chart
  • How many times greater? Counting the place-shifts
  • Division and multiplication sentences as the place-value step
  • Ten times or ten more? Correcting the claim
