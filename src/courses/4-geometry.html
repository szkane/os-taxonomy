🎯 What you’ll learn

  • ⬡Positional Language — Describe the position of objects using terms such as above, below, beside, in front of, behind, next to
  • ⬡3-D shapes — Recognise and name common 3-D shapes (cubes, cuboids, pyramids, spheres, cylinders, cones)
  • ⬡2-D shapes — Recognise and name common 2-D shapes (circles, triangles, rectangles including squares)
  • ⬡Combining Simple Shapes — Compose simple shapes to form larger shapes (e.g. two triangles make a rectangle)

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Positional LanguageTreats a position word as a fixed feature of the object rather than a relation to a landmark ('the front', with no reference to what it is in front of)Next step: Always pair the word with a named landmark and physically move the viewer or the object to show that 'behind' changes with viewpoint.
3-D shapesApplies a flat 2-D name to a solid, or classes a solid object as flat: a ball or coin is a 'circle', a box is a 'square'Next step: Use the 'can you pick it up and turn it over?' test; rotate the object to show it is round/solid all over and has thickness; re-anchor to the everyday twin plus the formal name (ball = sphere).
2-D shapesOnly the 'proper' equilateral, point-up triangle counts as a triangle; long-thin, right, obtuse or point-down ones are rejected ('that's too thin/pointy the wrong way to be a triangle')Next step: Show many varied triangles together and count the three straight sides on each; run 'is it or not?' with the child saying which feature makes each one a triangle.
Combining Simple ShapesChooses pieces by matching side LENGTH but not ANGLE, so pieces leave gaps/overlaps or a corner won't fit the space.Next step: Talk about the SPACE to be filled — the corners/angles of the gap — and choose a piece whose corner matches, comparing the angle of the gap to the angle of the piece.

Activity ideas

  • Place the object: follow spoken positional instructions
  • Your turn to tell me: give the instruction (barrier / role-flip)
  • In-role conversation: answering 'where is it?'
  • Build left and right on the body first
