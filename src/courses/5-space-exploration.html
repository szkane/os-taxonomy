📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is beginning their journey into space science — learning about astronauts and the Moon's phases, identifying objects in the sky, and discovering that we live in a solar system with other planets.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Sun, Moon & Starsthe Moon only comes out at night ('it can't be in the sky in the daytime')Next step: Go outside on a day when the Moon is up, or show photographs of a pale daytime Moon; establish that the Moon is simply present in the sky on many days, day or night.
Our Solar SystemEarth isn't a planet — it's the ground we live on, or the centre of everything ('planets are those other things out in space')Next step: Use a zoom-out model: ground → Earth as a whole ball → Earth as one of several balls circling the Sun; let the child watch Earth 'become' a planet.
What Astronauts Dothere's no gravity in space, that's why they float ('gravity stops once you leave Earth')Next step: Explain there IS gravity in space — it's exactly what keeps the Station circling Earth; astronauts float because they are falling around Earth together with the Station, not because gravity is gone.
Moon Phasesthe Moon looks like a sliver because clouds are covering the rest of it ('the missing part is hidden by cloud')Next step: Point out the dark part isn't grey cloud, it's simply the part we can't see lit; a Moon diary shows the shape follows the same monthly pattern whatever the weather.
Spotting Constellationsthe stars in a constellation are joined together or clustered close in spaceNext step: Show the stars sit at very different distances and only line up into the shape when seen from Earth; from another viewpoint the pattern would fall apart.

Activity ideas

  • Day sky / night sky: name what you see
  • Our sky diary: watch and record
  • Sort and compare: day, night, or both
  • Quick check: which one is it?
