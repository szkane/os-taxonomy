Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Rainforest Futures & Trade-Offsjust ban it — one law would fix everything ('the government should make deforestation illegal and it would stop')Next step: Role-play the stakeholders — a smallholder, an indigenous leader, a government, a consumer — and show whose needs a blanket ban would ignore and why it couldn't be enforced across borders.
Rainforests & Global Climatecutting a forest just removes shade / trees ('you lose the trees but the carbon isn't going anywhere')Next step: Show that the carbon locked in wood and soil is released as CO2 when the forest is burned or rots, so clearing actively ADDS to warming, not just removes a benefit.
Deforestation Causes & Scaledeforestation is mostly firewood / poor locals — 'villagers cutting trees for cooking'Next step: point to the large commercial drivers (cattle, soy, palm, logging, mining) clearing land for profit and export
Rainforest Conservationconservation means removing all people and locking the forest away ('the only way to save it is to keep everyone out')Next step: Show the evidence that indigenous-managed territories have lower deforestation than many official parks; reframe protection as securing the rights of people already caring for the forest.
Rainforest Products in Daily Lifemy shopping can't affect a distant forest ('it's on the other side of the world, nothing to do with me')Next step: Trace one concrete product — palm oil in biscuits, or soy fed to the chicken in a nugget — back to cleared land; show demand pulling supply.
Temperate Rainforestsrainforests exist only in the tropics near the Equator ('a rainforest has to be hot')Next step: Define a rainforest by rainfall and humidity, not temperature; show a map of temperate rainforest coasts and photos of moss-draped Atlantic oakwoods.
