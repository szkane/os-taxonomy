🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🔋Sunlight warms things up — Make observations to determine the effect of sunlight on Earth's surface, noticing that sunlight warms the ground, water, and objects
  • 🔋Building shade from the sun — Use tools and materials to design and build a structure that will reduce the warming effect of sunlight on an area, such as a shade or shelter
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is beginning to understand energy — observing how sunlight warms objects and surfaces, and designing simple structures to create shade and reduce warming effects.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Sunlight warms things upThe shade (or the cold) actively makes things cold, as if cold were a substance that comes out ('the shade sends cold onto the bench').Next step: Feel both benches, then move the shady one into the sun and watch it warm; there is no 'cold source', only more or less of the sun's warmth.
Building shade from the sunBuilds a structure but doesn't place it to block the sun's path — a wall beside the spot, or a roof full of gaps ('it's a shelter' but light still lands on the spot).Next step: Have the child look for the bright patch on the ground and move the cover until that patch is gone; shade is wherever the light is blocked.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Put two identical cups of water (or two thermometers) out on a sunny day — one in full sun, one in shade. FIRST ask the child which will be warmer after ten minutes AND why. …

Activity ideas

  • Touch the sun, touch the shade (firsthand outdoor observation)
  • Leave and revisit: two cups in sun and shade
  • Sunny-day sandbox (on-screen cause-and-prediction rehearsal)
  • Tap-to-reveal sunny scene (reference observation)
