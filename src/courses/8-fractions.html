🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍕Fractions of a whole — Understand a fraction 1/b as the quantity formed by 1 part when a whole is partitioned into b equal parts; understand a/b as a parts of size 1/b
  • 🍕Equivalent fractions on a number line — Understand two fractions as equivalent if they are the same size or the same point on a number line; recognise and show families of common equivalent fractions using …
  • 🍕Equivalent fractions (age 8+) — Generate simple equivalent fractions and explain why they are equivalent using visual fraction models
  • 🍕Fractions on a number line (age 8+) — Represent fractions on a number line: partition the interval 0 to 1 into b equal parts to locate 1/b, then mark off a lengths of 1/b from 0 to locate a/b
  • 🍕Decimal equivalents of tenths and hundredths — Recognise and write decimal equivalents of any number of tenths or hundredths (e.g. 3/10 = 0.3, 27/100 = 0.27)
  • 🍕Tenths (age 8+) — Count up and down in hundredths; recognise that hundredths arise when dividing an object by 100 or dividing tenths by 10
  • 🍕Decimal & Percent Notation — Read, write, and use decimal and percentage notation correctly — decimal, decimal point, tenths, hundredths, thousandths, percentage, per cent, % symbol, convert, …
  • 🍕Decimal place value (age 8+) — Compare numbers with the same number of decimal places up to two decimal places
  • 🍕Dividing by 10 and 100 — Find the effect of dividing a one- or two-digit number by 10 and 100, identifying the value of the digits as ones, tenths, and hundredths
  • 🍕Fraction-Decimal Equivalents — Recognise and write decimal equivalents of 1/4, 1/2, and 3/4
  • 🍕Adding Fractions (Same Denominator) — Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, including results greater than one whole (e.g. 5/8 + 6/8 = 11/8)
  • 🍕Comparing fractions (age 8+) — Compare two fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator by reasoning about size; record comparisons with >, =, or < symbols
  • 🍕Fractions of amounts (harder) — Solve problems involving increasingly harder fractions to calculate quantities, including non-unit fractions where the answer is a whole number
  • 🍕Decimals and fractions — Solve simple measure and money problems involving fractions and decimals to two decimal places
  • 🍕Fractions as parts of shapes — Partition shapes into parts with equal areas and express the area of each part as a unit fraction of the whole
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is developing a deeper understanding of fractions and decimals — learning to add and subtract fractions, convert between fractions and decimals, and use them to solve practical problems involving measurements and money.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Fractions of a wholeWhole-number bias — 'the second one is more because it has sixths and 6 is bigger than 4'Next step: Return to identical wholes; overlay one sixth on one fourth so the child sees the sixth is the smaller piece; only then re-ask the comparison
Equivalent fractions on a number lineSame-point denied — 'the dots are in the same place but the numbers are different, so they can't be equal'Next step: Dual-label the single point (2/3 above, 4/6 below) and measure both lengths from 0 to show they match
Equivalent fractions (age 8+)Additive equivalence — makes '2/4' from 1/3 by adding 1 to top and bottomNext step: Place both on the strip/line to show the lengths differ; contrast +1 (shifts the amount) with ×2 (re-partitions the same amount)
Fractions on a number line (age 8+)Counts tick marks not intervals — reads the 4th mark as if the marks were the count, or makes 4 interior marks to show fourthsNext step: Shade each interval and count LENGTHS from 0 ('how many jumps of the same size?'); make explicit that b equal parts need b−1 interior marks
Decimal equivalents of tenths and hundredthsDrops the placeholder zero — writes 7/100 as 0.7 (or reads 0.07 as 7/10), mapping the numerator straight after the point regardless of the denominator.Next step: Use the place-value chart / hundredths grid to show 7 hundredths sits in the second place, needing a zero in the tenths place; contrast 3/10 = 0.3 with 3/100 = 0.03. Reteach that place fixes the denominator (hundredths).
Tenths (age 8+)Whole-number bias on the denominator: 'a hundredth is bigger than a tenth because 100 is bigger than 10'.Next step: Overlay one hundredth on one tenth so the hundredth is visibly tiny; re-establish that more, smaller parts means each is smaller. Reteach that a tenth is one of ten equal parts of the whole and a hundredth is one of a hundred.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Before cutting, show two identical bars. Ask the child to PREDICT and give a REASON: if I shade 2 parts of a bar cut into 4, and 2 parts of an identical bar cut into 6, which shows more chocolate — or the same? The reason stated before observing is the diagnostic part. …

Activity ideas

  • Cut it fair, then count the unit
  • How big is ONE part? (vary the whole and the denominator)
  • Predict, then check: comparing same-numerator fractions
  • Identify, match and construct across models (consolidation)
