Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Friction & Surfaces'The block stops because the push you gave it ran out, not because of the carpet.'Next step: Contrast the SAME push on two surfaces so the only difference is the surface — force the child to attribute the different distance to the surface. Reteach pushes as forces if the link is missing.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Offer two surfaces: kitchen foil crumpled then flattened (shiny but rough) and a sheet of matte-smooth card. Ask the child to predict which lets the block slide further and WHY. …

Activity ideas

  • Same object, different surfaces: a fair-test friction runway
  • Forces spectacles: drawing the invisible friction arrow
  • Grip and slip, and the warming you can feel
  • Shiny is not the same as slippery (predict–observe–explain)
