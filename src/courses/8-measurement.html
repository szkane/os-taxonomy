🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 📏Area by Tiling — Find the area of a rectangle by tiling it with unit squares and show that the result equals the product of the side lengths
  • 📏Area of rectangles by multiplication — Multiply side lengths to find areas of rectangles and represent whole-number products as rectangular areas
  • 📏Area and the distributive property — Use tiling to demonstrate the distributive property: the area of a rectangle with sides a and (b+c) equals a×b + a×c; use area models to represent the distributive …
  • 📏Perimeters of polygons — Solve problems involving perimeters of polygons: find perimeter from side lengths, find an unknown side length, and explore rectangles with same perimeter but different …
  • 📏Converting measurement units — Convert between different units of measure (e.g. kilometre to metre, hour to minute, minute to second, year to month, week to day)
  • 📏Halves and quarters (age 8+) — Generate measurement data by measuring lengths to the nearest half and quarter inch; display the data on a line plot with a scale marked in whole numbers, halves, and …
  • 📏Estimating and comparing money — Estimate, compare, and calculate different measures including money in pounds and pence
  • 📏Elapsed time problems — Solve word problems involving elapsed time by adding and subtracting time intervals in minutes, including using a number line
  • 📏Measuring Liquids & Masses — Measure and estimate liquid volumes and masses of objects using grams, kilograms, and litres; solve one-step word problems involving mass or volume
  • 📏Area of compound shapes — Recognise area as additive; find areas of rectilinear figures by decomposing into non-overlapping rectangles and summing their areas
  • 📏12-hour and 24-hour time — Read, write, and convert time between analogue and digital 12-hour and 24-hour clocks
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning to measure the world around them — calculating areas and perimeters, converting between different units like metres and kilometres, and solving time problems using both analogue and digital clocks.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Area by TilingAdds the side lengths instead of multiplying: gives 4 + 6 = 10 (the perimeter) as the area.Next step: Re-establish area as the covering squares by counting the tiles; contrast with the once-around edge; then rebuild the array and connect the count to multiplication.
Area of rectangles by multiplicationAdds the side lengths instead of multiplying: gives 7 + 9 = 16 (or a perimeter) as the area.Next step: Return to the tiled array — the area is rows of squares, so it is squares-in-a-row × rows; re-derive 7×9 from tiling before using the formula.
Area and the distributive propertyDistributes to only one addend — reads or writes 6×13 = 6×10 + 3, labelling the second region just '3' instead of '6×3' (drops the repeated factor)Next step: Return to tiles: show the second sub-rectangle is still 6 (or 5) rows tall, so its area is 6×3 not 3; label the repeated side on BOTH regions.
Perimeters of polygonsAdds only the two given adjacent sides — 'it's 8 and 5, so the perimeter is 13' — forgetting the opposite pairNext step: Have the child first LABEL every side (each unmarked side equals its opposite) before adding — the documented remedy; then re-add.
Converting measurement unitsConverts in the wrong direction — divides when going larger→smaller, or expects the smaller-unit answer to be a smaller number ('3 km, so a bit less than 3 metres')Next step: Anchor on the inverse relationship — a km is big, a metre small, so one km holds MANY metres; larger→smaller multiplies and the number grows. Show the same length against a metre scale and a km scale so the differing counts are visible.
Halves and quarters (age 8+)Spaces the fraction marks unequally or out of order — puts ½ midway but crowds ¼ and ¾, or orders 2¼, 2¾, 2½Next step: Reteach partitioning one unit into equal parts with a fraction strip; align each ¼ tick under the ruler and check order 2¼ < 2½ < 2¾

Activity ideas

  • Build the array and watch the multiplication grow
  • Tile and label on squared paper
  • Area is not the distance around (spot the error)
  • Row-strips that fill the rectangle
