📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering advanced measurement skills — calculating volume and area using formulas, converting between different units of measurement, and solving real-world problems involving length, mass, and capacity.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Area of Triangles & ParallelogramsUses the slant side instead of the perpendicular height, believing every number given must be used.Next step: Mark the right angle and show the height must meet the base at 90°; give tasks with unnecessary numbers so 'given' does not mean 'needed'. Re-secure rectangle/parallelogram area first.
Volume as additiveChooses overlapping pieces and double-counts the shared region.Next step: Shade the two parts to show they must not overlap; assign the shared region to exactly one piece and recount.
Miles & KilometresAdditive rather than multiplicative reasoning — 'two more miles means two more km', or adds a constant to both quantities instead of scaling the ratio.Next step: On a double number line show that 15 miles = 3 × 5 miles, so the km must also triple (8 → 24); run a spot-the-mistake where adding a constant is shown to break the pattern. Reteach multiplication as scaling, not repeated addition.
Measurement ConversionsCompares without a common unit — decides 2450 ml is more than 2.5 l because 2450 > 2.5, treating the two measures as bare numbers.Next step: Always convert to one unit first: put both on a common-unit number line (2500 ml vs 2450 ml) and compare then; rehearse 'you can only compare like with like'.
Decimal place value'Add/remove a zero' with decimals — divides 3250 by 1000 by deleting zeros ('325 kg'), or multiplies 0.45 by 1000 by appending/one-step zeros ('45 m' or '4.5 m').Next step: Move digits three places on a place-value chart with the point fixed; use true/false items (0.45÷10 = .45? 25.34×10 = 25.340?) to show the zero shortcut fails for decimals.
Volume of cuboidsApplies V = l×w×h rotely with no sense of layers (Strategy D).Next step: Rebuild from layers with cubes — base area × height — so the formula means 'one layer, so many times high'.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Show the two rectangles (3×4 and 2×6) and a length of string. Ask the child to PREDICT whether the same loop of string will go exactly around both — given they cover the same area — and to say WHY before testing. Then lay the string around each: the 3×4 needs 14 cm, the 2×6 needs 16 cm. …
