  • 🥚Short division with remainders — Divide numbers up to four digits by a one-digit number using short division (and place-value/array strategies); interpret remainders appropriately for the context

  • 🥚Multiplying and dividing — Multiply and divide whole numbers and those involving decimals by 10, 100, and 1000

📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering more complex multiplication and division — working with larger numbers, understanding factors and multiples, solving multi-step problems, and beginning to use formal written methods.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Long multiplicationomits the placeholder zero — multiplies by the tens digit as if it were ones (34×2 recorded as 68, not 680)Next step: connect to the area model: the 34×20 region is twenty rows, worth 680, not 68; write the zero first to reserve the ones place, saying 'we are multiplying by twenty'.
Short division with remaindersdoes not carry the remainder into the next column — divides each digit independentlyNext step: model the carry explicitly: the leftover from each column becomes tens of the next; write the small carried digit before dividing the next place.
Division with remaindersapplies a fixed remainder rule regardless of context — always rounds up (6 teams) or always drops it (5 minibuses, stranding 7 children)Next step: ask 'what happens to the 7 leftover children in THIS story?' — force the meaning of the remainder in the specific situation to drive the rounding.
Factors, multiples, and primesthinks all odd numbers are prime (or all primes are odd) — calls 97 prime 'because it's odd', and would call 9 or 15 prime tooNext step: counterexample: 9 is odd but 3×3, so not prime; and 2 is even but prime. Primeness is about number of factors, not parity.
Multiplying and dividing'add zeros' rule — for 3.45 × 100 appends zeros to give 3.4500 instead of 345Next step: use the place-value chart: multiplying by 100 makes every digit worth 100× more, so each digit SHIFTS two columns left; the '3' becomes 3 hundreds. The point does not move — the digits do.
Multiplicative Comparisonreads 'N times as many/tall' additively — interprets '4 times as tall as 4' as '4 more', giving 8Next step: build it: stack four separate red towers end to end to SEE four copies; contrast with '4 more' by adding just 4 cubes, so 'times as many' and 'more than' become physically different.

Activity ideas

  • Bridge the area model to the written method (34 × 27)
  • Secure 4-digit × 1-digit short multiplication (2,347 × 6)
  • Map each written partial product to its area region (45 × 23)
  • Consolidation: spot-the-error and estimate checks
