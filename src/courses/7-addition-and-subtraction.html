🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍎Fluent adding and subtracting within 100 — Fluently add and subtract within 100 using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and the relationship between addition and subtraction
  • 🍎Fluent adding and subtracting within 20 — Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies; know from memory all sums of two one-digit numbers
  • 🍎Addition and subtraction within 1000 — Add and subtract within 1000 using concrete models, drawings, and strategies based on place value; understand composing and decomposing tens and hundreds
  • 🍎Adding and subtracting (age 7+) — Add and subtract numbers with up to three digits using formal written methods of columnar addition and subtraction
  • 🍎Missing number problems (age 7+) — Solve addition and subtraction problems including missing-number problems, using number facts, place value, and more complex methods
  • 🍎Two-Step Word Problems — Solve one- and two-step word problems within 100 using addition and subtraction, with unknowns in all positions
  • 🍎Addition and subtraction strategies (age 7+) — Explain why addition and subtraction strategies work, using place value and the properties of operations
  • 🍎Numbers on a number line — Represent whole numbers as lengths on a number line and represent sums and differences within 100 on a number line diagram
  • 🍎Mental addition and subtraction (age 7+) — Mentally add and subtract a three-digit number and ones
  • 🍎Adding numbers — Add up to four two-digit numbers using strategies based on place value and properties of operations
  • 🍎Mentally adding hundreds to 3-digit numbers — Mentally add and subtract a three-digit number and hundreds
  • 🍎Mentally adding tens to 3-digit numbers — Mentally add and subtract a three-digit number and tens
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is advancing their calculation skills — learning to add and subtract larger numbers up to 1000, mastering mental math strategies, and solving more complex word problems.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Fluent adding and subtracting within 100Smaller-from-larger subtraction bug — 'eighty-three take twenty-five: five take three is two', answering 62 regardless of column orderNext step: Go back to place-value regrouping with base-ten blocks: 83 must give up a ten to make enough ones (as 70+13) before the ones can be taken. Confront by checking 62+25.
Fluent adding and subtracting within 20No make-ten or derivation for crossing-ten sums — counts on by ones (8 + 7 → 9…10…15).Next step: Rehearse make-ten and near-doubles as bridges so crossing-ten facts become fast derived facts.
Addition and subtraction within 1000Smaller-from-larger across a column — in 402−137 does 7−2 in the ones ('seven take two is five') rather than exchangingNext step: Return to concrete decomposition within 100 first (this exchange is the same move one column up), then rebuild 402−137 with blocks so the child must break a unit open before subtracting.
Adding and subtracting (age 7+)Place-value misalignment — writes the digits ragged so, say, the 5 of 185 lines up under the 4 of 347Next step: Provide a column grid/headed H-T-O paper and rebuild; connect each column to its place-value counter so alignment has a reason, not just a rule.
Missing number problems (age 7+)Operator view of '=' — 'the answer comes after the equals', so 245 + □ = 380 is done as 245 + 380 = 625Next step: Use a balance/bar model to re-establish '=' as 'the same as'; both sides must match. Sort true/false equations to break the 'answer next' habit.
Two-Step Word ProblemsKeyword strategy — picks the operation from a cue word rather than the situation ('eaten'/'fewer'→subtract)Next step: Require a bar model first so the structure, not the word, drives the operation; use start-unknown and compare problems where the keyword misleads to discredit the shortcut.

Activity ideas

  • Check with the inverse and solve missing-number problems
  • Match the calculation to the smartest strategy (selection practice)
