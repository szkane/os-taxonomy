🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🏎️Gravity & Falling Objects — Explain that unsupported objects fall towards the Earth because of the force of gravity acting between the Earth and the falling object
  • 🏎️Force & Motion Vocabulary — Use technical vocabulary for force and motion — balanced forces, unbalanced forces, resultant force, acceleration, deceleration, speed, moment, lever, fulcrum, …
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning about gravity and forces that resist motion like friction and air resistance, while discovering how simple machines like levers and pulleys can make tasks easier.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Gravity & Falling Objects'The heavy stone lands first because gravity pulls harder on heavier things.'Next step: Drop two similar-shaped objects of very different mass (small vs large stone, or full vs empty bottle) side by side — they land together; then explain the pull-vs-harder-to-move balance.
Force & Motion Vocabulary'Going along at a steady speed means the forces are unbalanced — it's moving, so something's winning.'Next step: Steady speed = no change = balanced (zero resultant); reserve 'unbalanced' for changing motion.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Hold up a large stone and a small stone. Ask the child to predict which lands first and WHY, then drop them together — they land at the same time. For contrast, drop a flat sheet of paper against a crumpled ball of the SAME paper. …

Activity ideas

  • Air vs vacuum: strip the air and watch mass stop affecting how fast things fall
  • Predict-observe-explain drops with real objects
  • The Moon case: gravity without air
  • Ask an astronaut: why do astronauts float?
