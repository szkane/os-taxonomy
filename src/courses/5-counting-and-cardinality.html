🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍪Rote counting to 100 — Rote count forwards and backwards from 0 to 100, beginning from 0, 1, or any given number, by ones
  • 🍪Counting objects to 20 — Count a set of objects to answer 'how many?' for sets up to 20 (arranged in lines, arrays, circles, or scattered)
  • 🍪Counting in 2s — Count in multiples of 2, 5, and 10 (skip counting)
  • 🍪Seeing parts within 10 — Use subitising to recognise and name the parts of a collection up to 10 and combine them — e.g. …
  • 🍪Subitising to 5 — Recognise and name the number of objects in a collection of up to 5 instantly, without counting one by one (subitising), using dot patterns, dice faces, fingers and …
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is building essential counting skills — learning that numbers have meaning, that each number represents a specific quantity, and beginning to compare groups to see which has more or fewer objects.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Counting objects to 20count-out overrun — asked for exactly 13, counts past it or hands over the whole pile; doesn't use 13 as a stop signalNext step: state the target, count into a cupped hand, and stop-and-check — 'how many do we need? tell me when to stop'; this rebuilds the last-word-as-total link
Counting in 2sskip-counting-as-chant — recites the sequence as a memorised rhyme but can't say what comes after a given multiple without restarting, and can't map it to the numbersNext step: link each step to a group — a jump of 2 on the number line, a column of 10 on the square — saying 'each hop adds two' so the sequence carries meaning

Activity ideas

  • Predict across the decade boundary on a hundred square
  • Start anywhere and keep going
  • Daily rhythmic oral counting and take-turns buddy counting
  • Fill the gap and fix the sequence (retrieval practice)
