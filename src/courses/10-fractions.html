📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering advanced fraction operations — adding, subtracting, and multiplying fractions with different denominators, dividing with fractions, and solving real-world problems using visual models and mathematical reasoning.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Decimals and fractions (age 10+)Reverses the fraction-as-division: 'I divide the big number by the small one, so 3/8 is 8 ÷ 3 = 2.67' — divides larger by smaller out of whole-number habit.Next step: Return to the meaning of the bar: 3/8 is 3 things shared among 8, which must be less than one, so it is 3 ÷ 8. Model with 3 pizzas shared by 8 people before re-doing the division.
Simplifying FractionsSimplifies by subtracting the same amount from top and bottom ('take 6 off each: 18/24 becomes 12/18') instead of dividing — treats the fraction additively.Next step: Put 18/24 and their subtracted answer on the same bar/wall — the lengths differ, so the value changed. Re-establish that only ×n/n or ÷n/n leaves the quantity unchanged; reteach the equivalence principle.
Fractions of a whole (age 10+)'You can't share 3 among 4 — there aren't enough bars', so declares it impossible or flips it to 4 ÷ 3 = 1⅓Next step: Return to the sharing engine: cut each of the 3 bars into 4 and deal them out; everyone gets 3 quarters, so 3 ÷ 4 is defined and equals 3/4. Contrast with 4 ÷ 3 to show direction matters.
Area with FractionsReads one tile as 1/7 (or 1/(b+d)): 'three cuts and four cuts, that's seven pieces' — adds the partitions instead of multiplying themNext step: Go back to the physical grid: count the tiles in ONE unit square out loud (3 across, 4 down → 12) before naming any tile; connect 3×4 to the tile count and to the denominator of the product in mt_AabJisinfi
Multiplication as scaling'Multiplying always makes the number bigger, so 3/4 × 7 is more than 7' — the primitive multiplication-as-enlargement intuition.Next step: Reframe as 'three-quarters OF 7' and show on a stretch model that a <1 multiplier shrinks; ask when multiplying DOES make bigger to surface the multiplier>1 condition.
Dividing fractions (unit fractions)Gets 1/12 but cannot justify it — says the four pieces are still 'thirds', or cannot explain where 12 comes from (rule without the double-partition meaning)Next step: Build the partition physically — cut a shaded third into 4, extend the cuts across the whole, count the 12 — and connect 3 × 4 = 12 to the fraction-as-division meaning of the pieces

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Before dividing: 'I'm going to work out 3/8 as a decimal. First predict — will it be more than 1 or less than 1, and roughly what? Say why.' A child holding fraction-as-division predicts less than 1 (3 shared among 8) near a third-ish; a child reversing the division predicts more than 1 (8÷3). …
