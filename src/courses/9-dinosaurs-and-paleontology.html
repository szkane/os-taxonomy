📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring how scientists study dinosaurs through fossils — learning about dinosaur classification, evolution into birds, extinction events, and how paleontologists uncover and interpret evidence from millions of years ago.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Changing Scientific KnowledgeIf scientists change their minds they were wrong before, so they can't be trusted.Next step: Reframe revision as evidence-driven improvement — ask what new evidence appeared and why the newer view is better supported.
Reading CladogramsThe two species drawn next to each other are the most related.Next step: Trace each pair back to its branching node; relatedness is the recency of the common ancestor, not nearness along the row of tips.
Birds Evolved from DinosaursAll dinosaurs are extinct, so birds can't be dinosaurs.Next step: Establish birds as surviving theropod dinosaurs; the non-avian dinosaurs died out but one lineage lives on as birds.
Palaeoart & SpeculationThe picture shows exactly what the dinosaur looked like, colour and all.Next step: Sort features into evidenced (bones, size, feather traces) versus speculative (colour, patterning); colour rarely fossilises.
Rock Layers & Relative DatingReading the layers tells you the exact age in years.Next step: Distinguish relative dating (order) from absolute dating (a number); the strata give sequence, not years.
How Palaeontologists WorkJust dig it out fast, with big tools like any other digging.Next step: Stress that fossils are fragile and unique; show how a rushed dig shatters the specimen and destroys the surrounding evidence.

Activity ideas

  • Interview a palaeontologist who changed their mind
  • Weigh the evidence and watch the picture shift
  • Then-vs-now: tap to reveal what changed and why
  • Match the evidence to the idea it changed
