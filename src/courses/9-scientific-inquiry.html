🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🔎Evidence Supporting Ideas — Identify scientific evidence that has been used to support or refute ideas or arguments, evaluating the strength of evidence
  • 🔎Drawing conclusions from evidence (age 9+) — Report and present findings including conclusions, causal relationships, explanations, and a degree of trust in results using oral and written forms
  • 🔎Controlling variables — Plan different types of scientific enquiries to answer questions, recognising and controlling variables where necessary
  • 🔎Fair testing (age 9+) — Use test results to make predictions and set up further comparative and fair tests to investigate new questions
  • 🔎Science Can Be Revised — Scientific knowledge is provisional — it is the best current explanation based on available evidence, and it can and should be revised when better evidence arrives
  • 🔎Recording data and results — Record data and results of increasing complexity using scientific diagrams, classification keys, tables, scatter graphs, bar and line graphs
  • 🔎Accurate Measurement — Take measurements with increasing accuracy and precision using a range of scientific equipment, taking repeat readings when appropriate
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Evidence Supporting IdeasConfirming instance = proof: 'Aisha's is fine — her seed sprouted, so the food works.'Next step: Ask what Aisha compared against; with no no-food group, the sprouting could be coincidence.
Drawing conclusions from evidence (age 9+)Result treated as certain proof: 'the plants were taller, so more fertiliser definitely causes taller growth — done.'Next step: Introduce degree of trust: how many plants? were readings repeated? were other causes ruled out?
Controlling variablesAccepts the confounded conclusion: 'yes, it was the sunlight — the sunny one grew taller'.Next step: Highlight the two other differences (water, pot); ask which one caused it — establish that with three differences you can't tell.
Fair testing (age 9+)Restates existing data instead of extrapolating: predicts one of the heights already tested, or just repeats 'higher rolls further' with no new value.Next step: Ask 'which height haven't we tried yet? what would you expect there?' to push the prediction past the collected points.
Science Can Be RevisedWhen scientists change their minds it means they were wrong before, so science can't be trusted ('they keep changing it, so how do we know anything?').Next step: Reframe: revising in the light of evidence is what makes science trustworthy, not what breaks it; contrast with a belief that never checks itself.
Recording data and resultsChooses the wrong graph type for the data — e.g. a bar chart for continuous time data, or joins categorical bars into a line.Next step: Sort data as categories versus continuous first, then map each to its graph type; re-choose before drawing.

Activity ideas

  • Debate the claim out loud
  • Sort, rank, and compare evidence across claims
  • Claim-versus-evidence check (seed sprouting)
