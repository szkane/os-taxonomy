🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🏎️Predicting Motion Patterns — Make observations and measurements of an object's motion to provide evidence that a pattern can be used to predict future motion
  • 🏎️Drawing Force Diagrams — Draw and interpret force diagrams showing forces as labelled arrows — where the arrow's length represents the force's magnitude and its direction shows which way the …
  • 🏎️Balanced & Unbalanced Forces — Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence of the effects of balanced and unbalanced forces on the motion of an object
  • 🏎️Levers, Pulleys & Gears — Recognise that some mechanisms including levers, pulleys, and gears allow a smaller force to have a greater effect
  • 🏎️Air Resistance & Friction — Identify the effects of air resistance, water resistance, and friction, and understand that these forces act between moving surfaces to oppose motion
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is investigating how forces affect movement — observing patterns in how objects move and conducting experiments to understand how balanced and unbalanced forces change an object's motion.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Predicting Motion Patterns'I'll just guess how far — I don't need the earlier measurements for that.'Next step: Ask the child to point on their own results to what makes them expect that distance; require the prediction to sit on the pattern.
Drawing Force Diagrams'A still object has no forces, so I'll draw no arrows.'Next step: A still book has weight down AND the table pushing up, equal and opposite; balanced does not mean absent.
Balanced & Unbalanced Forces'If a force is pulling it, the forces must be unbalanced — even before it moves.'Next step: Point out the trolley isn't moving, so the pull is being cancelled (by friction / the held string) — that is balanced; unbalanced is when the motion CHANGES.
Levers, Pulleys & Gears'The machine gives you the extra force for free — you get more out than you put in.'Next step: Show the effort end travels much further than the load rises — force is multiplied, distance is traded; nothing is free.
Air Resistance & Friction'They'll land together — air pushes the same on everything, whatever the shape.'Next step: Fair-test flat vs crumpled (same paper), and a big vs small parachute, with repeat readings; air resistance grows with area.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Give the child a ruler-lever on a pencil pivot and a heavy object. Ask them to predict whether it's easier to lift by pushing near the object or far from the pivot, and WHY, before trying both. …
