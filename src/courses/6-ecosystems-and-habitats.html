🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🏞️Living, Dead & Never Alive — Explore and compare the differences between things that are living, dead, and things that have never been alive
  • 🏞️Habitats & Basic Needs — Identify that most living things live in habitats to which they are suited and describe how habitats provide for basic needs and how organisms depend on each other
  • 🏞️Habitat Vocabulary — Name and use vocabulary for where living things are found — habitat, environment, microhabitat, conditions, woodland, ocean, desert, rainforest, pond — and use terms to …
  • 🏞️Simple Food Chains — Describe how animals obtain their food from plants and other animals, using the idea of a simple food chain, and identify different sources of food
  • 🏞️Local Plants & Animals — Identify and name a variety of plants and animals in their habitats, including microhabitats such as under a log or in a pond
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring how living things interact with their environment — learning about different habitats, simple food chains, and the difference between living, dead, and never-alive things.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Living, Dead & Never Alive'Only things that move are alive, so the plant isn't living' — judges life by movement (animism's flip side); may also call the sun, fire or a river 'alive'.Next step: Reground on the full set of life processes (grows, needs water/light, makes new plants) using the vocabulary prerequisite; show time-lapse of a plant growing and turning to light so movement-free life is visible.
Habitats & Basic Needs'It'll be fine, it'll just get used to it' — believes an animal can live anywhere and adapts to any place within its lifetime; places seen as interchangeable.Next step: Return to what living things actually need to survive (the prerequisite), then match each need to what the pond supplies and the field lacks; a place is only a habitat if it meets the needs.
Habitat VocabularyUses 'habitat' to mean the single home or den — 'the crab's habitat is its shell/hole'.Next step: Contrast the whole rockpool (habitat) with the one crack the crab hides in (its shelter/microhabitat); the habitat holds many creatures, not one.
Simple Food Chains'The arrow shows who eats who, so it points from the fox to what it eats' — reads the arrow as 'eats' and reverses the whole chain.Next step: Rename the arrow out loud as 'is eaten by / food goes to'; walk energy from grass into the rabbit into the fox so the arrowhead follows the food.
Local Plants & Animals'They could live on the wall too, they just haven't gone there' — thinks creatures are distributed at random and could be found anywhere.Next step: Return to habitats-provide-needs (prerequisite), then make it local: what does damp/dark give a woodlouse that hot/dry cannot? Predict where each creature is BEFORE looking.
