🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🍎Adding and subtracting (age 8+) — Add and subtract numbers with up to four digits using formal written methods of columnar addition and subtraction
  • 🍎Two-Step Equations — Solve two-step word problems using the four operations; represent problems using equations with a letter standing for the unknown quantity
  • 🍎Two-step addition and subtraction problems — Solve addition and subtraction two-step problems in contexts, deciding which operations and methods to use and why
  • 🍎Fluent adding and subtracting within 1000 — Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and the relationship between addition and …
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering more complex addition and subtraction — working with larger numbers up to four digits, solving multi-step word problems, and using formal written methods alongside mental strategies.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Adding and subtracting (age 8+)Cannot decompose to subtract in 4002-1367 because the hundreds and tens are 0 (borrow-across-zero) - writes a garbled answer or stalls at the zerosNext step: Model exchanging one thousand downward through the columns with base-ten blocks (thousand to ten hundreds to ten tens to ten ones) BEFORE touching the digits.
Two-Step EquationsTreats the letter as the object: 'n stands for pencils / packs' rather than for a number we do not yet knowNext step: 'n is HOW MANY are in a pack - a number we haven't found yet.' Use the bar/box to show a numeric slot; contrast with 7.
Two-step addition and subtraction problemsWord-spots: 'more than means add, altogether means add everything' - does 340 + 85, or adds all the numbers, and stopsNext step: Draw the bar - who has MORE, Sam or Kim? Build the comparison model so the child sees Kim must be found by subtraction. Attack keyword reliance directly.
Fluent adding and subtracting within 1000Sets up 300-298 in columns and grinds a borrow-across-zero instead of noticing the two numbers are almost the same ('you just do the column method for take-aways')Next step: Put the two numbers on an empty number line and ask 'how far apart are they?'; contrast the time each method took. Build the habit of glancing at the numbers before choosing.

Activity ideas

  • Cascade the thousand: borrow across two zeros with base-ten quantities
  • Align and compose: mixed-length columnar addition and subtraction
  • Check it two ways: estimate and inverse
