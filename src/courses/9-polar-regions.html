Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Polar Conservation & Futureit's simple — just ban everything and it's solved ('why don't they just make it a rule?')Next step: surface the stakeholders and the no-owner problem; rules need many nations to agree and keep to them
Climate Change at the Polesmelting is a one-off, steady change — 'it just melts a bit more each year because it's a bit warmer', with no loopNext step: draw the loop as a circle (less ice -> darker surface -> more heat absorbed -> less ice) and contrast with a straight line
Earth's Frozen Waterall melting ice raises sea level — 'the sea ice at the North Pole melting is what floods coasts'Next step: run the glass demo; floating ice displaces its weight so the level holds; only land ice adds water
Polar Oceans and World Climatewarm water sinks / hot things go down — 'the cold water floats on top, the warm sinks'Next step: density demo (cold dyed water sinks under warm); cold + salty = densest, so it sinks
Polar Climate Zonedeserts are always hot and sandy — 'a desert can't be freezing, deserts are like the Sahara'Next step: redefine desert by precipitation; compare annual snowfall in the Antarctic interior with Sahara rainfall
Antarctic Treaty & Researchsomeone owns Antarctica — the country that discovered it or planted a flag can do what it likes ('Britain/America owns that part so they can mine it')Next step: show the treaty froze all territorial claims; no country owns Antarctica and decisions are made together
