📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning to be a young scientist — asking questions, making careful observations, recording what they see in simple charts and drawings, and testing ideas to find answers.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Recording DataDisorganised or unlabelled record: writes numbers with no indication of which car they belong to.Next step: Pair each result with its car — a row or column per car so the record is readable later.
Comparing Design SolutionsJudges by appearance or preference rather than by how each performed in the test ('the blue one looks stronger, it wins').Next step: Withhold the verdict until both are tested; ask 'what did the marbles actually show?' Anchor the decision to the results, not the look.
Modelling with SketchesDraws a decorative, detailed picture (colours, patterns, a happy bird) rather than a model showing how the shape solves the problem.Next step: Refocus on function: 'Which part actually stops the squirrel? Draw just that big.' Value the working feature over decoration.

Activity ideas

  • Live tally-and-table capture
  • Read the record and choose how to record
  • Parts of a tally chart (reference scaffold)
  • Best Design Wins: testing two bridges with a shared load
