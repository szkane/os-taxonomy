🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🌋Ring of Fire — Recognise that volcanoes and earthquakes tend to happen in certain places — especially around the edges of the Pacific Ocean (Ring of Fire) — not randomly across the …
  • 🌋Earth's Layers — Know that Earth has layers — a thin outer crust, a thick hot mantle, and a core at the centre — and that the inside of the Earth is extremely hot
  • 🌋Why Earthquakes Happen — Understand that earthquakes happen when rocks underground suddenly move or break, releasing energy that shakes the ground
  • 🌋Types of Rock — Know the three main types of rock — igneous (formed when lava or magma cools), sedimentary (formed from layers pressed together), and metamorphic — and that fossils are …
  • 🌋Inside a Volcano — Understand the inside of a volcano: magma is hot melted rock underground, lava is the same material after it reaches the surface, and volcanoes have a magma chamber, …
  • 🌋Active, Dormant & Extinct — Classify volcanoes as active (could erupt any time), dormant (sleeping but could wake up), or extinct (will not erupt again)
  • 🌋Pompeii & Vesuvius — Know the story of Pompeii: a Roman city buried by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD, preserved under volcanic ash, and rediscovered by archaeologists centuries …
  • 🌋Tsunamis — Know what a tsunami is: a very large, fast ocean wave caused by an earthquake or volcanic eruption under the sea, which can cause great damage when it reaches land
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning about what's inside volcanoes and earthquakes, how Earth has different layers, and exploring dramatic geological events like the story of Pompeii and tsunamis.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Ring of FireVolcanoes and earthquakes happen randomly, anywhere on EarthNext step: Confront with the plotted dots — trace the bands and the ring; they cluster along plate edges.
Earth's LayersThe crust is thick — a big chunky layer about the same as the othersNext step: Show a to-scale slice — the crust is a hair-thin skin on a huge hot interior.
Why Earthquakes HappenEarthquakes are caused by the weather ('earthquake weather' — hot, still days)Next step: Confront it — earthquakes come from rock movement underground, not weather; no weather causes them.
Types of RockFossils can be found equally in any type of rockNext step: Link formation to survival — heat and melting destroy fossils; only gentle layered sedimentary rock preserves them.
Inside a VolcanoMagma and lava are two different materialsNext step: Follow one blob up the vent — it's the same melt, renamed the instant it crosses the surface.
Active, Dormant & ExtinctIf a volcano hasn't erupted for a long time or looks green, it's extinct and safeNext step: Confront with a real case (a long-silent volcano that erupted) — long quiet means dormant, still a risk.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Loop an elastic band around a small block or brick sitting on rough sandpaper. Ask the child to PREDICT what the block will do as you slowly and steadily pull the far end of the band — will it slide smoothly and evenly, or something else, and WHY? …
