📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is becoming a young paleontologist — learning how fossils form, what they tell us about ancient life, and discovering fascinating facts about dinosaurs and the scientists who study them.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Fossils Reveal Ancient EnvironmentsThe animals were carried or climbed up: 'a big flood/wave carried the shells to the top.'Next step: Shift attention from the animal to the environment: the rock at the top formed on an ancient sea bed, then rose.
How Fossils FormFossilisation is automatic and common: 'when animals die their bones just slowly turn into fossils over time.'Next step: Contrast the millions of animals that have died with the few fossils found; make burial-before-decay the pivotal step, using a rotting-in-the-open counter-image.
Fossilised Dinosaur DungDung is too soft or gross to fossilise: 'poo can't turn into a fossil, so this isn't real evidence.'Next step: Establish that dung can be mineralised and preserved like other trace fossils; the hardened contents are exactly what makes it useful.
Types of FossilsOnly bones and teeth are 'real' fossils: 'a footprint isn't a fossil.'Next step: Widen the definition of fossil to include preserved traces of activity; a footprint turned to rock is as much a fossil as a bone.
Reading Dinosaur TrackwaysConflates stride with size: 'the far-apart prints mean the biggest dinosaur.'Next step: Separate the two variables explicitly — print size tells you body size, the gap between prints tells you speed; check each independently.
The Mesozoic EraAll dinosaurs lived at the same time: 'they were all around together, so they'd have met.'Next step: Lay the three periods on a timeline and place Stegosaurus and T. rex tens of millions of years apart; different dinosaurs, different eras.
