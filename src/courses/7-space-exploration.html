📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is discovering how our solar system works — learning about Earth's movements that create day and night, exploring the eight planets and their features, and understanding how we use robots and telescopes to study space.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

The Sun is a starthe Sun is not a star — it's its own special kind of thing ('stars are the little night lights, the Sun is something else')Next step: Line the Sun up alongside the night stars as the same kind of object; the only difference is nearness. Revisit that the Sun is a sky object first.
Earth's Spin & Orbitthe Sun travels across and around the Earth to make day and nightNext step: Spin a globe beside a fixed lamp and 'ride' a figure on it — the child sees the lamp only APPEARS to move because the globe turns; the Sun stays put.
The Eight PlanetsPluto was destroyed, blew up, or flew out of the solar systemNext step: Clarify Pluto is still orbiting the Sun exactly as before — only the naming rule changed in 2006; it's now called a dwarf planet.
Space Robots & Roversthe rovers are driving around Mars to find aliens living there nowNext step: Explain rovers look for CLUES in the rocks that water once flowed and that tiny life could ever have existed — evidence, not creatures walking about.
Planet Featuresthe planet closest to the Sun must be the hottest ('Mercury has to be hottest because it's nearest')Next step: Introduce the atmosphere-as-blanket idea: Venus's thick gases trap heat while airless Mercury lets it escape; temperature depends on more than distance.
The Moon's Orbitthe Moon's phases are caused by the Earth's shadow falling on the MoonNext step: Use a lamp and a ball: the lit half always faces the lamp, and walking the ball around 'Earth' reproduces every phase with NO shadow involved; name the rare eclipse as the real shadow event.

Activity ideas
