Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Comparing Arctic & Antarctic'Both poles are the same — frozen sea under the ice' — no land-vs-ocean distinction.Next step: Two cross-sections: Arctic = ice on OCEAN; Antarctic = ice on thick LAND. Show Antarctica's rock beneath kilometres of ice.
Polar Food Chains'Food-chain arrows point to the prey / to what gets eaten' — reverses the energy-flow convention.Next step: Fix the rule: the arrow means 'is eaten by' / 'energy goes to' — it points to the EATER. Re-read the chain aloud with that rule.
The Race to the South Pole'Scott just had bad luck / Amundsen got lucky' — outcome attributed to chance, not choices.Next step: Line up the choices side by side (transport, depots, experience); show how they shaped speed, energy and safety margins.
Ice & States of Matter'All ice is the same' — no distinction between sea ice and glacial ice.Next step: Sort by origin: sea ice = frozen ocean (salty, thin); glacial ice = compressed snow on land (fresh, thick); icebergs = broken-off glacier.
The Arctic Tundra'Treeless = lifeless / empty frozen desert' — no trees read as no life.Next step: Show the summer tundra bursting with mosses, lichens, flowers and grazing caribou; treeless doesn't mean barren.
Inuit & Sami Peoples'Inuit/Sami are people from long ago who no longer exist' — relegates living peoples to history.Next step: Present tense, present day: show contemporary Inuit and Sami communities keeping traditions alongside modern life; they are here now.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)
