🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🪐How fossils form — Identify evidence from patterns in rock formations and fossils in rock layers to support an explanation for changes in a landscape over time
  • 🪐The solar system — Describe the sun, Earth, and moon as approximately spherical bodies, and describe the movement of the Earth and other planets orbiting the sun in the solar system
  • 🪐Earth's rotation and day/night — Use the idea of the Earth's rotation to explain day and night and the apparent movement of the sun across the sky
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring our solar system and Earth's place in it — understanding how the Earth's rotation creates day and night, how planets orbit the sun, and how we can read Earth's history through rocks and fossils.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

How fossils form'the top layer is the oldest — it's been sitting there longest' (inverted superposition)Next step: Build layers by dropping sediment — each new layer lands ON TOP, so the bottom formed first; oldest at the bottom, youngest at the top.
The solar system'the Sun goes round the Earth' (geocentric model, reinforced by sunrise/sunset language)Next step: Run a Sun-centred model beside an Earth-centred one and test which matches what we see; anchor 'the Sun is at the centre, Earth is one of the planets.'
Earth's rotation and day/night'the Sun travels round the Earth once a day — that's why it rises and sets' (geocentric, from sunrise/sunset language)Next step: Fix a lamp as the Sun and spin the child or a globe; the moving horizon makes the fixed Sun appear to travel — the child feels the apparent motion is theirs.

Activity ideas
