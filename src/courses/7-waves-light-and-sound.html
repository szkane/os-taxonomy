How the world works

💡 Central idea (suggested)

Sound comes from vibrations and light travels from sources — their strength and direction change what we hear and see.

🧭 Lines of inquiry (suggested)

  • What exactly is waves, light & sound?
  • How do “How Shadows Form” and “Volume & Vibrations” connect?
  • How can we use waves, light & sound in our own lives?

🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🥁How Shadows Form — Recognise that shadows are formed when light from a source is blocked by an opaque object, and find patterns in how shadow size changes
  • 🥁Volume & Vibrations — Find patterns between the volume of a sound and the strength of the vibrations that produced it
  • 🥁Reflecting Light — Notice that light is reflected from surfaces, and that shiny smooth surfaces reflect light best
  • 🥁Pitch of Sounds — Find patterns between the pitch of a sound and features of the object that produced it
  • 🥁Protecting Eyes from Sunlight — Recognise that light from the sun can be dangerous and that there are ways to protect eyes
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is discovering how light behaves — learning that sunlight can be dangerous to eyes, understanding how shadows form when light is blocked, and observing how different surfaces reflect light.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

How Shadows FormShadow as a reflection/copy: 'the shadow shows the toy's front, its face and colours, like a copy'Next step: A shadow is where light is BLOCKED, so it can only be a dark outline; contrast it directly with a mirror, which DOES show the toy's detail and colour.
Volume & VibrationsVolume and pitch collapsed: 'hitting harder makes it higher'Next step: Independent moves — vary hitting force (volume) on the same drum so pitch is fixed, then change the drum's tension/size (pitch) at a fixed force; prove louder is not higher.
Reflecting LightOnly shiny surfaces reflect: 'dull things don't reflect, only mirrors and shiny stuff do' — often 'reflection means a mirror image'Next step: This is largely instruction-induced (all examples were mirrors) — deliberately investigate shiny AND dull surfaces; link 'you can see it' to 'light bounced off it'; expect resistance and parallel school-versus-real-world explanations.
Pitch of SoundsPitch and volume collapsed: 'the tighter band is louder' — higher and louder are the same 'more'Next step: Dissociate with independent moves — change tension (pitch) while keeping plucking force (volume) fixed, then change force at fixed tension; prove that louder is not the same as higher.
Protecting Eyes from SunlightProtection equals permission: 'sunglasses (or squinting) make it safe to look right at the sun'Next step: Sunglasses reduce glare for comfort but never make direct sun-gazing safe; the safe move is not to look at all. Separate 'protects your eyes generally' from 'lets you stare at the sun'.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Predict what the shadow of this toy on the wall will look like — will you see its face and colours, or just an outline? Then predict whether the shadow gets bigger or smaller when you slide the toy toward the lamp. Say why for each before we try, then we test it and you explain.

Activity ideas

  • Resize the shadow: a shadow is blocked light, not a copy
  • Measured fair test: the distance–size pattern
  • Predict-explain-test: outline or copy?
  • Diagnose prior ideas and check understanding
