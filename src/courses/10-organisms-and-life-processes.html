🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🦋Evolution vocabulary — Use technical vocabulary for evolution and natural selection — adaptation, evolution, natural selection, extinct, extinction, fossil record, species, common ancestor, …
  • 🦋Diet, Exercise & Lifestyle — Recognise the impact of diet, exercise, drugs, and lifestyle on the way human bodies function
  • 🦋The Circulatory System — Identify and name the main parts of the human circulatory system and describe the functions of the heart, blood vessels, and blood
  • 🦋Nutrient Transport in Animals — Describe how nutrients and water are transported within animals, including the role of the circulatory system in delivering nutrients from digestion
  • 🦋Energy from Food & the Sun — Use models to describe that energy in animals' food was once energy from the sun, transferred through plants or other organisms
  • 🦋Plants Grow from Air & Water — Support an argument that plants get the materials they need for growth chiefly from air and water, not from the soil
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring how living things get and use energy — from understanding how plants make food from sunlight and air, to learning about the human circulatory system and how lifestyle choices affect our bodies.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Evolution vocabularyuses 'adaptation' for the whole process instead of for the feature ('the moths did an adaptation to survive')Next step: fix 'adaptation' = the feature and 'natural selection' = the process that spreads it; rehearse both in one narrative
Diet, Exercise & LifestyleExercise only makes your visible muscles bigger — nothing changes insideNext step: The heart is a muscle too and gets stronger with exercise; compare a fit and an unfit person's resting pulse to make the internal change visible.
The Circulatory SystemThe heart makes new blood to replace blood that gets used upNext step: The heart pumps; blood recirculates and is not used up; blood cells are made in bone marrow. Separate 'pump' from 'produce'.
Nutrient Transport in AnimalsFood travels through the gut and tubes straight to where it's neededNext step: Nutrients are absorbed into the blood at the small intestine wall; the blood, not the gut, delivers them to cells. Link the two systems explicitly.
Energy from Food & the SunThe energy comes from the food itself — the meat — full stopNext step: Follow the chain one link at a time back to the Sun; ask 'and where did THAT get its energy?' at each step.
Plants Grow from Air & Waterplants eat the soil, so mass comes from soil ('the soil turns into plant')Next step: Present the mass-balance data (plant +kg, soil −grams). The soil can't be the source — the mass came from air and water. This is the classic van Helmont result.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Have each student measure their resting pulse, do one minute of vigorous activity (star jumps), then measure again and time how long it takes to return to resting. Ask them first to PREDICT what the pulse will do and why. …
