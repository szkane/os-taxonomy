🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧱The three digits of a three-digit number — Understand that the three digits of a three-digit number represent amounts of hundreds, tens, and ones
  • 🧱Ordering Numbers to 1000 — Compare and order numbers up to 1000 using >, =, and < symbols, based on place-value understanding
  • 🧱The multiples of 100 — Understand that the multiples of 100 (100–900) each represent a number of hundreds with 0 tens and 0 ones
  • 🧱Representing numbers to 1000 — Identify, represent and estimate three-digit numbers using different representations: place a number on a 0–1000 number line marked only in hundreds or unmarked, …
  • 🧱Reading and writing numbers to 1000 — Read and write numbers to 1000 in numerals, number names, and expanded form
  • 🧱10 or 100 More or Less — Find 10 or 100 more or less than a given number up to 1000
  • 🧱Place Value to 1000 — Solve number problems and practical problems involving place value of numbers up to 1000
  • 🧱Odd or Even — Determine whether a group of objects (up to 20) has an odd or even number of members
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering larger numbers — learning to read, write, and work with numbers up to 1000 by understanding how hundreds, tens, and ones work together.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

The three digits of a three-digit numberTreats the placeholder 0 as meaningless and collapses the number: reads 706 as 76Next step: Build 706 with 7 hundred-flats, no tens, 6 ones; the empty tens spot IS the zero. Contrast 76, 706, 760.
Ordering Numbers to 1000Compares from the ones, or by a bigger digit anywhere, instead of hundreds-first: judges 254 vs 245 by the ones (says 245 is bigger)Next step: Compare place by place from the hundreds; stop at the first place that differs (tens: 5 > 4).
The multiples of 100Treats the zeros as meaningless and collapses the number: '300 is the same as 3'Next step: Build 300 with 3 hundred-flats, no tens, no ones; the empty tens and ones ARE the zeros. Contrast 3, 30, 300 on a place-value chart.
Reading and writing numbers to 1000Concatenates the spoken chunks into digits: writes 30042 (or 300402) for 'three hundred and forty-two'Next step: Build 342 with base-ten blocks and write it into a hundreds/tens/ones frame so each part lands in one column, not appended.
10 or 100 More or LessAdjusts the wrong place — adds 100 to the tens digit, or adds 10 to the hundredsNext step: Name which column '10 more' and '100 more' act on; add the matching base-ten piece and watch only that column (until it overflows).
Place Value to 1000Believes a number has only ONE correct decomposition ('350 IS 3 hundreds and 5 tens, that's it')Next step: Model several equivalent builds of 350 with base-ten pieces (exchange a hundred for ten tens) and name each as the same number seen differently.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Give the child 13 counters. Before touching them, ask: 'If you pair these two-by-two, will they all get a partner, or will one be left over?' Have them commit to a prediction and say why, THEN pair to check. …
