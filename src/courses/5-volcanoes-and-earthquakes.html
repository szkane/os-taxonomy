🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🌋What Is a Volcano — Know what a volcano is: an opening in Earth's surface where hot melted rock (lava) comes out
  • 🌋What Is an Earthquake — Know what an earthquake is: a sudden shaking of the ground that can be strong or weak
  • 🌋Earth Is Made of Rock — Understand that Earth is made of rock and other solid materials
  • 🌋Fast & Slow Earth Changes — Understand that Earth's surface changes: some changes are quick (eruptions, earthquakes) and some are slow (wind, water wearing away rock)
  • 🌋Power of Eruptions — Appreciate that volcanic eruptions are powerful events that can change the landscape
  • 🌋Earthquake Safety — Know basic earthquake safety: drop, cover, and hold on; move away from windows; tell a trusted adult
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning about powerful forces that shape our Earth — understanding what volcanoes and earthquakes are, how they change the landscape, and important safety rules to follow during earthquakes.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

What Is a VolcanoThe orange stuff is fire — the volcano is burning ('the mountain's on fire')Next step: Confront it — melt then cool a solid (wax/chocolate): glowing means hot, not burning, and lava cools into hard rock.
What Is an EarthquakeEvery earthquake is huge and knocks everything down (doesn't know they vary in strength)Next step: Most earthquakes are so weak we don't even feel them; introduce a strength range from barely-felt to very strong.
Earth Is Made of RockIt's dirt or soil all the way downNext step: Soil is a thin skin — dig deeper and you hit solid rock (bedrock); show a soil-over-rock cross-section or a photo of digging foundations.
Fast & Slow Earth ChangesOnly big dramatic events change the land; gentle water or wind can't change solid rockNext step: Show worn stone steps or a smooth round pebble — slow change adds up over very many years.
Power of EruptionsAn eruption only destroys — the land is dead and ruined foreverNext step: Show before / just-after / years-later pictures; ash enriches the soil and plants return.
Earthquake SafetyRuns outside as soon as the shaking startsNext step: Don't reteach the words — model that the most dangerous place is next to exterior walls and windows; stay and take cover until shaking stops.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Show the child a solid piece of wax or chocolate. Before doing anything, ask them to PREDICT: if we heat it until it flows like lava and then let it cool, will it (a) burn up into ash, (b) turn into fire, or (c) become solid again — and WHY? …

Activity ideas

  • Press to erupt
  • Melt-and-cool prediction (hands-on check)
