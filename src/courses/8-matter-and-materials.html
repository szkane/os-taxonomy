📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring states of matter — learning to identify solids, liquids, and gases, and discovering how water changes form through evaporation and condensation.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Evaporation & the Water CycleEvaporated water 'disappears' or 'is used up' — the water is thought to cease to exist rather than becoming vapour.Next step: Reconnect to change of state: liquid water becomes an invisible gas that mixes into the air; trap it (cover a warm dish) and watch it recondense to prove it's still there.
Solids, Liquids & Gases'Pours means liquid' — classifies powders and grains as liquids because they flow.Next step: Examine one grain under a magnifier — a hard solid with a fixed shape; contrast the pouring PILE with the fixed GRAIN.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Put equal amounts of water in two identical dishes; leave one on a warm sunny sill and one in a cool shady spot (optionally cover a third with cling film). Ask the child to PREDICT which empties fastest, WHERE the water goes, and why, before waiting a day. …

Activity ideas

  • Follow one tagged water drop around a manipulable cycle
  • Cold-glass close-up: where does the condensation come from?
  • Labelled water-cycle diagram with reveal flaps (vocabulary scaffold)
  • Hands-on investigation: warm versus cool dish, with a covered dish and a sealed-bag mini cycle
