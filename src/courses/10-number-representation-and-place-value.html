🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧱Place Value × 10 and ÷ 10 — Recognise that in a multi-digit number, a digit in one place represents 10 times as much as in the place to its right and 1/10 of what it represents in the place to its …
  • 🧱Reading and writing numbers (age 10+) — Read, write, and compare decimals to thousandths using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form; compare using >, =, < based on place-value meaning
  • 🧱Reading Decimal Places — Identify the value of each digit in numbers given to three decimal places (e.g. in 4.378, the 7 represents 7 hundredths)
  • 🧱Numbers to Ten Million — Solve number and practical problems involving reading, writing, ordering, comparing, rounding, and negative numbers up to 10,000,000
  • 🧱Reading and writing numbers to 10,000,000 — Read, write, order, and compare numbers up to 10,000,000 and determine the value of each digit
  • 🧱Decimal place value — Round decimals to any place using place-value understanding; round any whole number to a required degree of accuracy
  • 🧱Measuring temperature — Use negative numbers in context (temperature, finance, sea level); calculate intervals across zero
  • 🧱Patterns with Powers of Ten — Explain patterns in zeros when multiplying by powers of 10 and in decimal-point placement when multiplying/dividing by a power of 10; use whole-number exponents to …
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is mastering large numbers and decimals — reading, writing, and comparing numbers up to 10 million, understanding how decimal places work, and using negative numbers in real-world contexts like temperature and money.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Place Value × 10 and ÷ 10Face value — 'they're the same, both digits are 4', ignoring position.Next step: Confront with place-value counters or a Gattegno chart: slide the 4 from tenths to hundredths and show its worth divides by ten; the digit is the same but its place is not.
Reading and writing numbers (age 10+)'Longer decimal is larger': says 0.372 > 0.38 because 372 > 38 / it has more digitsNext step: Align by place (tenths under tenths); compare tenths first (3 vs 8). Pad with a zero (0.380) to see equal-length strings.
Reading Decimal PlacesReads the decimal part as a whole number — 'the 7 is worth 7', treating .378 like three hundred seventy-eight.Next step: Rebuild the places with a decimal place-value chart; read 4.378 as '4 ones, 3 tenths, 7 hundredths, 8 thousandths', anchoring each column to a fraction.
Numbers to Ten MillionRounds using the wrong place — looks at a later digit or rounds 8,274,500 up to 9,000,000 because 'the rest is big', instead of reading the hundred-thousands digit (2).Next step: Underline the millions digit and box the single digit to its right; state the rule 'only that one digit decides'. Model on a number line between 8,000,000 and 9,000,000.
Reading and writing numbers to 10,000,000Period/grouping error — misreads 4,302,561 (e.g. 'four hundred thirty thousand...') or writes it as digits with wrong grouping, especially around the internal zero.Next step: Rebuild the number in a period-labelled place-value grid (millions | thousands | ones); read each three-digit group then attach its period name.
Decimal place valueTruncates instead of rounding: chops 3.4567 to 3.45 rather than 3.46Next step: Look at the digit just past the cut (6 ≥ 5 → round up). Place 3.4567 between 3.45 and 3.46 on a number line to see which is nearer.

Activity ideas

  • Slide the same digit across the places
  • Why isn't it just 'add a zero'?
  • Compare across many places and across zero
  • Explain and defend your reasoning
