Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Simple Chance ExperimentsExpects the small experiment to match the theoretical split exactly and concludes the coin is biased when it does notNext step: Do not reteach the mechanics — pool their flips with others' to reach 100+ and show the proportion settling near half; name that 20 trials is too few to judge fairness (the seed of the experimental-vs-theoretical topic)
Likelihood LanguageUses 'even chance' for any situation with two outcomes ('it's 50-50, there's red and blue') regardless of how many of each there areNext step: Confront directly: count them together — 7 red vs 3 blue is not equal, so it is not an even chance; ask what the bag would need for 'even chance' to be true (equal numbers)

Activity ideas

  • Predict, flip, tally: run and read a coin experiment
  • Watch the proportion settle: scale up the number of trials
  • The coin has no memory: confront recency thinking
  • Interpretation check: does a small sample prove bias?
