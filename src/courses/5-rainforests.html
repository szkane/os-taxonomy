Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

What Is a Rainforest?hot places are dry like a desert — 'it's hot so it must be dry and sandy'Next step: contrast a desert (hot, dry) with a rainforest (hot, wet, rains daily); show it's the rain that makes it green
Rainforest Layersthe forest floor is bright and sunny — 'it's outdoors so it must be light'Next step: the dense canopy roof blocks the light; the floor is dim; light lives at the top
Rainforest Animalsall animals live together on the ground — no sense of layersNext step: revisit the layers; match climbers/fliers to the canopy and ground-hunters to the floor
Rainforest Plantsepiphytes are parasites — 'the orchid steals food or water from the tree'Next step: epiphytes only perch for light and make their own food; they don't feed on the tree
Where Rainforests Arerainforests could be anywhere — no link to the EquatorNext step: point to the Equator belt on a globe; a rainforest needs hot and wet, which happens near the middle
Indigenous Rainforest Peoplesindigenous peoples lived long ago / are 'primitive' — 'they're from history, not now'Next step: use the present tense; they are living communities and expert forest scientists in their own right
