Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Insects in ecosystemsSees insects mainly as pests/nuisances — thinks losing them would be good or make little differenceNext step: Confront with a concrete chain the child values: no bees → no apples/strawberries; no dung beetles → dung and dead matter pile up. Make the hidden services visible before judging insects as pests.
The most successful animals on EarthConfuses many INDIVIDUALS with many SPECIES — thinks 'lots of insects' means lots of the same few kindsNext step: Separate the two ideas: a million ants in one nest is one species; a million species means a million DIFFERENT kinds. Count kinds, not bodies. Compare '# of species' vs '# of individuals' explicitly.
Threats to insects and conservationSees insects as pests — thinks fewer insects is a good thing, not a problemNext step: Reconnect decline to services they value: fewer pollinators → less fruit; fewer insects → hungry birds. Weigh a few nuisance species against the many essential ones before judging the decline good.
Insect AdaptationsLamarckian: thinks individual moths changed their own colour to match the sooty trees during their lifetime ('they turned dark to hide')Next step: Stress that a single moth is born its colour and keeps it for life — it cannot repaint itself. Track two colour variants across several generations: only the PROPORTIONS change, because survivors pass colour on. Contrast 'the moth changed' with 'which moths survived changed'.
Insect communication and behaviourThinks the dance is just random movement or excitement, carrying no informationNext step: Show that the dance is systematic: change the food's location and the dance angle/length changes too. A meaning that reliably maps to location = information, not excitement.
Types of MetamorphosisThinks ALL insects go through complete metamorphosis — assumes every insect has a caterpillar/larva and a pupaNext step: Contrast two life cycles side by side: butterfly (young totally different, has pupa) vs grasshopper (young is a mini-adult, no pupa). Establish that a pupa is NOT universal.
