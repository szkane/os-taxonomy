🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🧊Conservation of Mass — Measure and provide evidence that the total weight of matter is conserved regardless of the type of change (heating, cooling, or mixing)
  • 🧊Matter Is Made of Particles — Develop a model to describe that matter is made of particles too small to be seen, and that this explains properties of solids, liquids, and gases
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is exploring the fundamental nature of matter — learning that everything is made of tiny particles they can't see, and discovering that matter is conserved even when it changes form through heating, cooling, or mixing.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Conservation of Massburning destroys matter, so mass is lost ('the wax is used up and gone')Next step: Track the atoms: wax + oxygen → CO2 + water vapour, all still in the sealed jar. Weigh a sealed reaction to show no drop. Confront the 'used up' idea directly.
Matter Is Made of Particleseach particle has the bulk material's properties ('a copper particle is orange and hard'; 'water particles are wet')Next step: Ask what colour a single particle could even be, and what's between particles — shift them to properties as arising from many particles arranged together, not stamped on each one.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Predict-Observe-Explain: put a fizzing tablet and water in a flask, first with the stopper OFF, then a fresh one with the stopper ON, each on a balance. Ask the child to predict each reading over time and WHY. A 'matter destroyed / gas weightless' model predicts a drop in both. …

Activity ideas

  • Mass detective: track the whole system
  • Consolidate: where did the mass go?
  • Predict and justify the balance reading
  • Predict–Observe–Explain: compare open and closed system data
