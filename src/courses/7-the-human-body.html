📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is discovering how their body works — learning about different types of teeth, how food travels through the digestive system, how bones and muscles work together, and understanding the importance of a balanced diet.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Cells, Tissues & OrgansThe body and its organs are NOT made of cells — cells are only in special places like blood, or only in plants.Next step: Establish that every part of the body is built from cells, including muscle, skin, and bone — not just blood.
The Digestive JourneyFood just travels through one tube and whatever isn't used comes straight out the other end — nothing crosses into the body.Next step: Focus on the small intestine wall as a crossing point: nutrients pass through it into the blood; only the leftovers continue as waste.
How the Eye WorksYour eyes send out rays or beams to see things (extramission).Next step: Reverse the arrow: light travels from lit or reflecting objects INTO the eye; the eye is a receiver, not an emitter. Use the dark room as proof.
Types of TeethAll teeth do the same job / tooth shape isn't linked to what an animal eats.Next step: Match shape to task with real examples — flat for grinding, sharp for tearing; have the child predict an animal's teeth from its food.
How Muscles Move BonesA single muscle pushes the bone back to straighten the arm (muscles push as well as pull).Next step: Reaffirm muscles only pull; introduce the triceps as the partner that pulls the arm straight — an antagonistic pair.
Naming Major BonesAll animals have bones inside like us — a crab has bones inside its shell.Next step: Introduce the exoskeleton as a hard OUTER casing that replaces internal bones; the shell IS the skeleton, on the outside.
