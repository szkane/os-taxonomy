📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is discovering how their amazing body works — learning about the heart, lungs, brain, skeleton, and five senses, plus understanding what their body needs to stay healthy and strong.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

How Breathing WorksBreathed-in and breathed-out air are the same — you just push the same air back out unchanged.Next step: Establish that the body keeps some oxygen and adds carbon dioxide — so out-air differs; ask why we'd need to keep breathing if nothing changed.
The Heart & BloodThe heart only beats and blood only moves when you run or move about.Next step: Feel the pulse while sitting still to prove it beats now; explain it speeds up with exercise but never stops.
Basic Body Needssleep is just boring or 'doing nothing' — the body doesn't really need itNext step: name rest and growth as sleep's jobs and link a missed night to feeling tired and unable to concentrate
The Brain Controls the BodyThe brain is only for thinking or learning, not for seeing or moving the body.Next step: Broaden the brain's job: it also receives from the senses and commands the muscles; give movement examples, not just 'thinking'.
Bones & MusclesThe skeleton isn't important — bones don't do much and the body would be fine without them.Next step: Make the jobs concrete: bones give shape and hold you upright, and hard bones shield soft organs; picture a body with no frame.
The Five SensesTaste and smell are the same sense, or one organ does both.Next step: Separate them: the nose detects smells (odours in the air), the tongue detects tastes (flavours in food); they cooperate but are different senses.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)
