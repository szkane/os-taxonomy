How the world works

💡 Central idea (suggested)

Sound comes from vibrations and light travels from sources — their strength and direction change what we hear and see.

🧭 Lines of inquiry (suggested)

  • What exactly is waves, light & sound?
  • How do “Light & Seeing in the Dark” and “Light & Sound Vocabulary” connect?
  • How can we use waves, light & sound in our own lives?

🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🥁Light & Seeing in the Dark — Observe that objects in darkness can be seen only when illuminated by a light source
  • 🥁Light & Sound Vocabulary — Name and use vocabulary for how light and sound behave — light source, transparent, translucent, opaque, shadow, reflect, vibration, pitch, volume — and apply these …
  • 🥁Communication with Light & Sound — Design and build a device that uses light or sound to solve the problem of communicating over a distance
  • 🥁Vibrations & Sound — Understand that vibrating materials can make sound, and that sound can make materials vibrate
  • 🥁Transparent, Translucent & Opaque — Investigate the effect of placing objects made of different materials in the path of a beam of light, discovering transparent, translucent, and opaque materials
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is investigating light and sound — discovering how we see objects, experimenting with transparent and opaque materials, and learning how vibrations create sound.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Light & Seeing in the DarkDark adaptation: 'if I wait, my eyes get used to the dark and then I can see' — the tell is 'at night I can see my room after a while'Next step: Distinguish a truly sealed dark room from a dim room where light leaks in; do the light-tight-box or blindfold experience — you never adapt to genuinely zero light. Hunt for where the 'little bit of light' was sneaking in before.
Light & Sound VocabularyPitch/volume swap: says 'high volume' for loud or 'loud pitch' for high — the confusable pairNext step: Pitch = how high/low; volume = how loud/quiet; pin each to a separate change (tighten the string vs hit harder).
Communication with Light & SoundCommunication = just making a signal: flashes the torch or makes a noise with no agreed meaningNext step: Information transfer needs a shared code agreed in advance; judge success by whether the message arrives, not by whether a light or sound was produced.
Vibrations & SoundSound needs a visible vibration, so a hidden one is denied: 'there's no vibration, the sound came from hitting it hard' — the tell is 'I can't see it move so nothing moved'Next step: Run a recurring 'spot the vibration' routine; make invisible vibrations visible or felt (rice on a drum, tuning fork dipped in water, fingers on the throat); deliberately choose non-obvious sound sources.
Transparent, Translucent & OpaqueCollapses the middle category: only two groups — 'if any light gets through, it's transparent' (or 'if I can't see clearly through it, it's opaque')Next step: Contrast side by side — can you READ text through it (transparent) versus only see a glow with no image (translucent)? The missing middle is the whole teaching point.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Predict what you will see when we shut you inside the light-tight box (or put on the blackout blindfold) and wait two minutes — will the objects slowly appear? Say why first. Then try it and explain what actually happened.

Activity ideas

  • Dark den: does the light leave, or the dark arrive?
  • Dimmable scene: run the disappearance yourself
  • Sort: makes its own light vs needs light to be seen
  • Talk-it-through: scooping out the dark
