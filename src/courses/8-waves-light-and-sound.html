🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 🥁Sound Travels Through Materials — Recognise that vibrations from sounds travel through a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to the ear
  • 🥁Patterns & Codes for Information — Generate and compare multiple solutions that use patterns to transfer information, such as codes and signals
  • 🥁Sound Fading with Distance — Recognise that sounds get fainter as the distance from the sound source increases
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is discovering how sound works — understanding that sounds are vibrations that travel through air, water, and solids, and exploring how pitch and volume relate to the strength of these vibrations.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Sound Travels Through MaterialsSound only through air: 'you can only hear through air; a solid or water blocks the sound'Next step: First-hand hearing through a solid (ear on the table, tap the far end) and through water; sound travels through solids and liquids, often faster and better than through air.
Patterns & Codes for InformationAny signal communicates: 'if I flash the torch, she'll just understand' — signal and message conflatedNext step: A message arrives only if both sides share a CODE agreed in advance; test by whether the receiver decodes the intended meaning, not by whether a signal was sent.
Sound Fading with DistanceFading equals slowing: 'the sound slows down as it travels, so it gets fainter'Next step: Speed depends only on the medium, never on distance or loudness — a faint far sound and a loud near sound travel at the same speed. Hold speed constant while loudness drops.

Try at home (predict–observe–explain)

Predict: if you press your ear flat against the table while a friend taps the FAR end of the table (not through the air), will you hear it clearly, faintly, or not at all? Say why first, then try it and explain what carried the sound.

Activity ideas

  • Pick-a-medium sound bench (on-screen): swap the material between source and ear
  • String telephone: taut carries well, slack carries poorly
  • Ear to the desk and listening through water (first-hand media)
  • Why is space silent? The vacuum boundary case
