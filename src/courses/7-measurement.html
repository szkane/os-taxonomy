🎯 What you’ll learn

  • 📏Measuring length (age 7+) — Measure the length of an object by selecting and using appropriate tools such as rulers, yardsticks, metre sticks, and measuring tapes
  • 📏Calculating with measurements — Measure, compare, add, and subtract lengths (m/cm/mm), mass (kg/g), and volume/capacity (l/ml) using standard units
  • 📏Measuring & Plotting Lengths — Generate measurement data by measuring lengths to the nearest whole unit and display the data on a line plot
  • 📏Measuring Perimeters — Measure the perimeter of simple 2-D shapes
  • 📏Comparing Time Durations — Compare durations of events and calculate the time taken by particular events or tasks
  • 📏Time to the nearest minute — Tell, write and estimate the time to the nearest minute on analogue and digital clocks, using vocabulary such as o'clock, a.m./p.m., morning, afternoon, noon and …
  • 📏Addition and subtraction word problems — Solve word problems involving lengths within 100, using addition and subtraction with drawings and equations
  • 📏Time Units and Calendar Facts — Know the number of seconds in a minute and the number of days in each month, year, and leap year
  • 📏Quarter past and quarter to — Recognise, read and show quarter past and quarter to the hour on an analog clock, linking each to a quarter turn of the minute hand
  • 📏a.m. and p.m. times — Tell and write times to five minutes on analogue and digital clocks with a.m. or p.m. …
  • 📏Giving Change — Add and subtract amounts of money to give change, using both £ and p in practical contexts
  • 📏Money word problems — Solve word problems involving dollar bills, quarters, dimes, nickels, and pennies, using $ and ¢ symbols appropriately
  • 📏Comparing lengths by measuring — Measure to determine how much longer one object is than another, expressing the length difference in terms of a standard length unit
  • 📏Roman numeral and 24-hour clocks — Tell and write the time from analogue clocks with Roman-numeral faces (I to XII, including IV, which some clocks show as IIII, and IX), knowing that the minute positions …
  • 📏Measuring with different units — Measure the length of an object using two different length units and describe how the measurements relate to the size of the unit chosen
  • 📏Smaller units for accuracy — Choose a smaller uniform informal unit when a measurement needs to be more accurate, recognising that a smaller unit gives a larger count, for length, mass and capacity
📖 Goals, common misconceptions & printable ideas

Learning goal

Your child is learning practical measurement skills — using rulers and other tools to measure length, weight, and volume accurately, working with money, and telling time to the nearest minute.

Common misconceptions (from teaching research)

Measuring length (age 7+)Reads from '1' — numerals as start-signal: aligns the object's end with the '1' mark, or reports the last numeral crossed, so the count is off by one.Next step: Reteach that 0 is the start and that a numeral names the units of space already covered (the space up to the mark, not the mark); build or annotate a unit ruler to reconnect the numerals to iterated units.
Calculating with measurementsAdds/subtracts across different units as bare numbers: treats unlike units as like, e.g. 2 m + 30 cm = 32, or 1 kg + 200 g = 201.Next step: Insist on a common unit before calculating; show both quantities on one scale so the mismatch is visible; return to attending to the unit when measuring.
Measuring & Plotting LengthsReads the height of a stack as the length — 'this object is 3 long' because three Xs sit above itNext step: Slide the actual ruler under the plot and ask them to point to where an object of that length would sit; hold the two questions apart — 'how many objects' vs 'how long is one'
Measuring PerimetersAdds only the two given sides of a rectangle, forgetting the opposite two.Next step: Have the child label the lengths of the other two sides first (opposite sides equal), then add all four; trace the full boundary to check.
Comparing Time DurationsUses column subtraction (end minus start), which breaks when hours are crossedNext step: Model counting on via a number line, bridging to the next o'clock; show explicitly that the minutes are base-60, so borrowing 10 doesn't work.
Time to the nearest minuteReads the hour as the numeral the hour hand is nearest, so 7:43 becomes 8:43Next step: Emphasise 'the hour is the number the hour hand has LAST PASSED'; use a moveable clock to watch the hour hand travel between two numbers across the hour.
