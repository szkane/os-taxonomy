# STORYBOOK PAGES — a design philosophy

## "Stage-Lit Paper Theatre"

The picture-book page is a small theatre: a lit stage, a paper proscenium, actors who stand ON the ground — never float in void. Every page is composed as a diorama in three planes: a far horizon of soft hills, a near rolling ground with an inked contour, and foreground actors anchored by honest contact shadows. The craft standard is the printed picture book: warm paper, deliberate palette of four hues per tale, one warm light source, air and dust as quiet witnesses. Nothing is pasted on; everything is staged.

## Space and depth

Depth is built by layering, never by blur: sky wash → sun or moon → far hill in a paler tone → near hill in the tale's ground hue → actors sorted back-to-front so nearer figures overlap farther ones honestly. The horizon breathes on a curve, never a ruler line. Foreground grass ticks and scattered motifs give the ground a woven, handmade texture — the patient repetition of a printed page examined closely. Negative space is protected at the top of the stage so the sentence below never competes with sky business.

## Light and material

Each tale declares one palette; the renderer derives every tone from it — hill shades, shadow ink, motif accents — so no page can drift off-harmony. A single warm disc lights the scene (gold by day, pearl by night), and a barely-there vignette presses the edges inward like a pressed-flower book. Water tales ripple; night tales scatter stars; meadow tales grow grass. The material is paper: a cream frame inset, rounded corners, an honest drop shadow — the page sits ON the site, framed like a book held open.

## Composition and rhythm

Actors obey the rule of thirds: the protagonist holds a strong-side position, supporting cast answers across the middle, the light source occupies the opposite corner and alternates sides page by page — a heartbeat of change as pages turn. Each figure may lean a degree or two, a printed-page imperfection that reads as life. Scale is disciplined: one size system across the whole book, figures anchored at the ground line, shadows scaled to mass. Turning the page should feel like turning a page: same theatre, new blocking.

## The standard

Every page must look labored over: the seeded scatter placed as if by hand, the contours inked with a steady line, the palette harmonized to a single key. If a page could be mistaken for a sticker sheet, it has failed; if it could be torn from a printed book, it has succeeded.


## Nine stages — one per subject (cover backdrops)

The theatre travels: each subject sets its own stage, its own light, its own air.

- **Mathematics — Blueprint.** Cool graph-paper sky, faint ruled grid, floating rough circles/triangles/squares like construction sketches; props stand on a drafting shelf. No sun, no grass — geometry is the weather.
- **Science — Laboratory.** Mint air, a bench line, rising bubble chains and molecule dot-clusters wired by thin lines; props on the bench among the apparatus.
- **English — Notebook.** Warm ruled paper, a red margin line, ink flourish swirls, floating letters (A a B b) drifting like seeds; the shelf is a desk edge.
- **History — Parchment.** Sepia grain, two rough dune curves, a distant pyramid trio on the horizon; the sun is high and pale; props cast long desert shadows.
- **PSD — Sunrise Meadow.** Peach-to-rose sky low over green hills, hearts lifting on strings like balloons, soft and warm above all other stages.
- **Computing — Circuit Board.** Deep navy night, right-angle traces with glowing node pads, a subtle dot grid; the shelf is a chip edge; props glow against the dark.
- **Geography — The Map.** Parchment map sheet, a dashed expedition route ending in an X, a small compass rose, continent blobs; the props are placed like markers on the chart.
- **Life Skills — Kitchen Counter.** Warm cream, a checkered tile band, shelf silhouettes with jars, steam curls rising past the props.
- **Learning to Learn — Growth Garden.** Morning sky over tilled soil rows, sprouts in a rhythm, a staircase of steps rising to the light.

One discipline, one weather. The palette still derives from each course's artPalette so sibling courses stay related — but the STAGE tells you the subject before you read a word.
